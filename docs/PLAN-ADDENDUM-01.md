## 1. What changes (one page)

**The product.** Proxim stays a device-side decision core that never receives coordinates. It is a location-trigger and contextual-experience platform: Trigger (a map feature or drawn shape plus an event) -> Gate (consent, caps, safety, benefit) -> Delivery (in-app slot, inbox, or local notification) -> Experience (what opens on tap). Advertising ships first, as PLAN.md scopes it. A venue talking to its own customers (resort, estate, campus, car park) is "owner mode", built after the advertising pilot is live. The unit is still the "signpost"; detector, envelope, pack format, renderer, consent, sensitive-zone code and simulator are one implementation (2.5 says what is shared and what is two pipelines). Packs stay public, keyless and identical for all partners; the server never sees a coordinate; the SDK never asks for Always or background location; health signposts stay in-app only.

**What is new**
1. **Pick things on the map.** v1: a business, area, neighbourhood or road, or a drawn shape; buildings, gates, junctions, car parks, transit and import follow in Stage 1.1. The console says honestly what will fire (sections 3-4).
2. **Events.** The enum reserves enter, exit, cross, dwell, approach. v1 advertising uses enter and approach; cross is Stage 1.1 (foreground/partner-fed, claimed places, in-app); owner departure is an armed OS exit (4.1); dwell is Stage 3.
3. **Notifications, first-party only.** Local notifications, owner mode only (sender = the partner app's own organisation), service content, general-sensitivity places, always passive and title-only. Third-party adverts are never OS notifications (Apple 2.5.18, 4.10 [V]; Google [V-sec]); they keep the in-app slot and inbox.
4. **Guest-specific information is handed off, never stored.** Notification and guide are generic and readable by anyone with the owner's app; room, door code and booking are fetched after tap by the owner's app from its own backend behind its own login.
5. **"Beneficial" is a layered gate, not a model that watches the user:** declared intent classes, review-time AI on the sender's content, deterministic on-device rules, user controls, and the owner's own arming of which signposts apply to which guest. No per-user AI in Proxim and no decision callback into the owner's app.

**What is not possible**
- A gate, door or point cannot be detected point-precisely in the background: a ring of at least 150 m (Android) or 150-200 m (iOS) wakes the app minutes late. At 15 m/s the OS typically reports entry about 2.5 km (Android) to 3.9 km (iOS) past the boundary at the median and 5.9 / 9.6 km at the 95th percentile [MODEL, N1-N2]. Gate-level (3-5 s) detection exists only in foreground or partner-fed modes. Indoors and floor level are not decidable; in dense urban canyons boundaries are uncertain by about 50 m and "which of two close roads" is only 58-68% right [MODEL].
- Nothing is promised after force-quit or before first unlock. A posted notification cannot be recalled until the app next runs; iOS has no documented auto-expiry for delivered local notifications [U].
- A guest who has not opened the app inside the freshness window (N4) gets no arrival notification, by design; the in-app arrival guide is their path.
- Owner notifications work only where the venue is clear of sensitive buffers (rural and resort-type sites, 9.6) and only if Google approves background location for the partner's real app (8.4).
- Proxim cannot stop an owner's own app logging arrivals against a customer identity; it can only refuse to be that channel.

**Sequencing.** There is no "days of work" stage. Stage 0 is the generality tax inside M0-M5 (reserved fields with CHECKs, signed envelope v2, the v1 subset of `proxim-detect`): 6.5-12 person-weeks [U] (8.6). Stage 1 is advertising v1 (hosted, in-app slot and inbox, enter and approach, four pickers). Stage 1.1 (gate G-1.1, after M13) adds claims, micro-geometry, cross/pass, the other pickers, import, Drive-it v2. Stage 2 (owner mode, notifications, guides) has gate G-O0, starts only after the hosted pilot (M13) is live, and the store outcome G-O1 gates M16 (11.2). The rest is "Later" (8.2).

## 2. The platform model: Trigger -> Gate -> Delivery -> Experience

### 2.1 Naming (ADR-001)
**signpost** stays the unit; "Sponsored", rate card, CPM and quality score belong to `content_mode = hosted`. `content_mode` is `hosted` (third-party) or `owner` (first-party); console labels "Promote" and "Venue". `event` is the geometric transition (present, enter, exit, cross, dwell, approach); `intent` the benefit class (wayfinding, arrival_service, departure_service, event_info, transactional, safety_info, promotion, advertisement). Crate `proxim-events` keeps its meaning (telemetry "report events"); geometric "trigger events" live in a new pure crate `proxim-detect`. Reserved envelope/schema fields (values restricted until their milestone): `content_mode`, `event`, `intent`, `delivery`, `experience`, `audience_scope`, `venue_exposure_class`, `pack_kind`, `event_params`, `group_id`, `priority`, `map_ref`, `precision_class`, `min_mode`, `label_key`, `relation`, `arm_scope`, `selector`, `live_at`, `fresh_required_s`, `sz_sha256`.

### 2.2 The four stages
1. **Trigger** = shape + event. Shapes: `zone`, `gate` (oriented transect with inbound side), `point_pass`, `road` (corridor), `junction` (later), `approach`, `sequence` (<= 2 steps; reserved). Each carries `precision_class` (BG_RING >= 150 m, FINE, EXACT), `min_mode` (M1-M3), `max_acc_m`, hysteresis, `cooldown_s`, `max_event_age_s`, `max_event_dist_m`. The console reads allowed combinations from `capabilities(shape, event, mode)`. A kernel that meets an unsupported event ignores it fail-closed (`UNSUPPORTED_EVENT`).
2. **Gate** = ordered pure filters with reason codes. Universal: signature, expiry, seq, revocation and freshness, consent receipts, kill switch, child-directed, quality gate, sensitive-zone rules. Hosted: partner category/sensitivity policy, caps, V x Q x R x P (hosted background off by default, `HOSTED_BACKGROUND_OFF`). Owner: relation, `arm_scope`, intent whitelist, I13, `selector`. Plus schedule, caps by intent, benefit rules (section 6).
3. **Delivery** = ordered channels, falling back to a quieter one only while the app is in the foreground (a location wake never falls back to the inbox: it posts or does nothing, 5.3): `in_app_slot`, `inbox`, `local_notification`. No host callback channel (5.6).
4. **Experience** = `card` | `guide` | `deeplink` | `web`. A guide is at most 12 pages of structured blocks, no HTML or JS. A notification tap opens a `guide` or an allowlisted app deeplink only; `web` is reachable from the inbox or an in-app card (9.3a).

`decide(state, fix, packs, policy, consent, clock, seed)` returns `(decisions, state')`; trigger state is explicit so golden vectors cover enter->exit sequences.

### 2.3 Hosted versus owner
A relationship test, not a content test. A signpost is OWNER only if relation **O1** holds: the sender org is the partner org that owns the receiving app (one verified org record and KYB, plus the app signing identity: bundle id or package + certificate SHA-256). Everything else is HOSTED. The test runs at authoring, signing, compile and as a DB constraint; `relation` is in the signed envelope (I17); a hosted key cannot set `content_mode`.

**Decision (specialists conflicted):** v2.0 ships O1 only. The schema reserves O2 (same legal entity), O3 (parent/affiliate), O4 (managing agent), but `owner_binding.relations_enabled = ['same_org']` and no binding tables exist until Stage 3. If ever enabled (after counsel and a store pre-clearance on whether a different legal entity's service notice in the partner app is acceptable [U], COUNSEL): per-venue bindings signed by both orgs at aal2, tied to the app signing identity, at most 12 months, revocable; at most 5 bindings and 20 venues per app; service intents only; renderer-built label; human review of EVERY revision; in-app and inbox only for 90 days. Always HOSTED (in-app, inbox, "Sponsored", never an OS notification): tenants, concessionaires, sponsors, franchisees, licensees, resellers, agencies, advertisers, any paid placement. A mall, campus or airport app may host them as a PARTNER (and earn the share) but may not label them "From <tenant>".

Decisions recorded: owner bundle hard TTL is 7 d, not the 30 d a specialist proposed (longer weakens takedown). Owner reporting is zero events ("posted" is not "seen"; Path S cannot observe delivery). W_service is 72 h, not 24 h: the typical guest last opened the app at booking and 24 h delivers nothing in the flagship scenario; the cost (unsynced takedown up to 72 h, equal to the hosted hard TTL) is owner decision 4.

### 2.3a Owner bundle distribution: fetch-all
**Decision.** Every install of a partner app fetches the signed owner manifest and every blob it lists, armed or not, whatever the consent or booking state; arming is applied on the device. Proxim cannot tell an armed install from an unarmed one by request content or by anything `arm()` does; the timing of partner-called `refresh()` is a declared residual (I14b, I8c). A client that fetches only what it needs reveals it, and cover traffic is not independence, so per-venue, per-region or bucketed fetch is not offered.
- Routes `GET /o/v1/{app_pub_id}/manifest` and `GET /o/v1/blob/{sha256}` (content-addressed, no key), reserved at M5; no `/o` route takes a venue, signpost, region or bucket parameter or a query string (CI lint).
- Schedule: first fetch at `configure()`, then at foreground start (never a location relaunch, 5.2a) when the cached manifest is older than the bundle soft TTL (N4), plus the best-effort scheduled refresh (5.2a); a function of time and cache state only. `arm()`, `disarmAll()`, consent changes, trigger events and notification state never cause, suppress, delay or alter a request. Only the partner-wide CMP master switch may gate SDK network, identically for all of it. A partner-called `refresh()` sends the same requests, coalesced to at most one set per 6 h.
- Caps N10 (the SDK refuses an over-cap manifest; a venue whose sidecar exceeds its cap compiles without notification delivery and the console says why). Console text on every owner screen: "Anyone with your app can read every venue's content." Data-flow inventory addition: "Owner manifest and blob GETs: app id, IP, time, conditional-request headers; no location, arm state or consent state; identical in content for every install; request timing may reflect the partner's `refresh()` calls (I14b)." Enforced by a dependency-graph test (the fetcher cannot import the arm store) and egress-invariance tests (11.8).

### 2.4 How the privacy invariants and the billboard principle apply
**Billboard principle, hosted (clarified).** A signpost may depend on where the device is and is heading, the time, the partner's closed-taxonomy context, topics the user chose in the on-device Preference Center, and the user's suppress actions. A partner may suppress, never select or boost, via declarative flags with no returned value. It never depends on inferred user attributes, health inputs or identifiers.
**Billboard principle, owner (new, with justification).** A first-party signpost may depend on where the device is, time, and which of the owner's signposts its app has armed on this device (with an arm window). Proxim never receives, stores, derives or logs who the user is, what is armed, or that a trigger fired. The principle exists to stop third-party targeting and Proxim-side profiling; in owner mode the sender is the party that knows the customer and identity never crosses to Proxim. Arming is a list of ids from the owner's booking state, not a user attribute, and is never transmitted.

Status: **enforced** = a named passing test in v1; **design-rule** = no guarded code path may exist yet (`check-invariants`, 8.7).

**I8 (restated; (a) is PLAN's I8 verbatim).** (a) Location-derived requests (`/t/*`: path, query or body holds an H3 cell, tile id or other location-derived value) carry no publishable key, attestation token, cookie, bearer token or device/install/session identifier, and on web no Origin/Referer beyond what the browser forces (PLAN-REVIEW #11). (b) Credentialed requests (`/v1/config`, `/o/*`, `/v1/attest`, `/v1/events`, `/v1/reports`) carry no location-derived value; `/o/*` and `/v1/config` also nothing derived from arm, consent-purpose, trigger or notification state. This clause owns the content of `/v1/config` and `/o/*` requests (enforced in v1 for `/v1/config`, from M15 for `/o/*`); I14(b), a design-rule until Stage 2, adds only the schedule, order and size invariance of those requests. (c) Declared residual: until OHTTP (Phase 2), requests of classes (a) and (b) from one device share an IP and can be joined by anyone who sees both logs. Also declared: the timing of partner-called `refresh()` requests is the partner's choice and can correlate with bookings (I14b). An owner-only app makes no class (a) requests (its sensitive-zone data ships in the bundle), so it is strictly better than a hosted app; a mixed app has the join until OHTTP carries `/t` and `/o`.

**New invariants.** I13-I16, I19, I20 are `design-rule` until Stage 2; I17, I18, I2a are enforced in v1.
- **I13 Notification gate.** An OS notification is posted or scheduled only if: mode is owner; class is service (promo reserved, off); sensitivity is general; the signpost is bound to this app; its signature verifies at post/schedule time; the SDK consent receipt exists; OS permission is fully granted (provisional authorization does not count); the device is positively CLEAR of every sensitive buffer per the signed venue sidecar of the signpost's own bundle (missing, stale, tampered or incomplete data, or a fix outside coverage, is NOT clear); the freshness rules of N4 pass; caps pass. Only the kernel posts, at wake time, except optional Path S (5.2); third-party content is never posted by OS-scheduled triggers. **Path S clause:** for Path S the conditions are evaluated at schedule time only (signature, revocation-list age, consent, permission, caps, and the ring-clearance pre-gate: the whole OS region circle + 200 m outside every facility buffer + margin per the sidecar) and re-verified on tap; the fire-time conditions (sidecar CLEAR on a device fix, arm window) cannot be evaluated at post time, which is why Path S is owner-only, optional and flagged.
- **I14 Identity firewall.** (a) Proxim never receives, stores or forwards push tokens, user/guest identifiers, entitlements, arming lists, or per-device enter/exit/cross/dwell/arrival events; no presence-attestation API, webhook, SMS or email on trigger; no notification to anyone but the device user. No network request is initiated, enqueued or timed by a location-triggered wake; refresh is triggered only by foreground, the poll while active, scheduled work independent of location, and a partner call. (b) **Arm-independence:** the set, order, URLs, headers, sizes and schedule of requests to `/o/*` and `/v1/config` are a function of (partner app, SDK version, global content versions, local cache state, install-seeded schedule, clock and the partner's `refresh()` calls) and nothing else. The timing of partner `refresh()` calls is a declared residual (I8(c)): the SDK coalesces them to at most one fetch set per 6 h, and a `refresh()` with an unchanged cache sends exactly the requests a timer refresh would. (c) **Host output minimisation (owner mode):** no SDK-provided callback, stream or return value reaches host code on enter, exit, cross, dwell, approach, a Gate decision or a suppression; host-visible outputs are verified user-initiated taps, status states with no position or arrival information, and the card in the visible slot. **Declared residual (not prevented):** OS-visible facts in the host process (the launch reason of a location relaunch, the app's own delivered notifications, its own sandbox including the SDK ledger row and the arm record's `fired` mark) can reveal an arrival to the venue's own app; Proxim cannot hide them and does not claim to. (The hosted `SignpostDecision` stream is a pre-existing PLAN decision, accepted in section 6.)
- **I15 No confidential content in packs or bundles.** Packs and bundles are public by extraction: anyone with the app or its key can read them. They hold no confidential operational detail and, for Class B (restricted-access or residential) venues, no geometry finer than the registered ring; linted for secrets and operational detail; a CI byte-grep proves a Class B bundle holds no coordinate but the ring.
- **I16 Sensitive buffers.** No notification surface (OS notification, inbox write, scheduled or deferred item) is evaluated, queued or deferred inside a sensitive buffer, and none proceeds when the sidecar test returns NO_DATA (dropped, never deferred). Enter/exit/dwell triggers are refused where geometry plus hysteresis margin touches a buffer; no `zone_state` is written there. Confidential-address kinds are never on a device; no owner notification signpost may exist whose posting envelope touches a confidential barred cell. Order is normative (5.3): nothing is written until the test is CLEAR; the one fix taken for it is memory-only and discarded on IN_BUFFER or NO_DATA.
- **I17 Signed envelope.** The signature covers geometry and event parameters (`precision_class`, `min_mode`, `max_acc_m`, `cooldown_s`, `max_event_age_s`, `max_event_dist_m`), event types, `content_mode`, delivery, `experience`, text, label key, `intent` (wherever this document says `purpose`, it means `intent`), `venue_exposure_class`, `aud`, `audience_scope`, sensitivity class, `relation`, `arm_scope`, `selector`, `sz_sha256`, `live_at`, `fresh_required_s`, `pack_kind` and expiry; the signer refuses an early `live_at` reading the database independently of the API.
- **I18 Micro-geometry.** Triggers below the 150 m floor exist only under a verified, feature-bound place claim over a public-access, non-residential feature. In v1 none can exist for any sender.
- **I19 Visit state.** `zone_state` exists only for owner dwell (Stage 3; not built in v1 or the Stage 2 pilot): 15-minute buckets; TTL 12 h default, 24 h hard max (unchanged); encrypted, excluded from backup; wiped on withdrawal and `eraseAllLocalData`; never uploaded; bounded. No exit, departure or cap logic may read it.
- **I20 Revocation.** Withdrawal, kill switch and revocation (scopes: signpost+rev, signpost, sender_org, kid) remove pending and delivered Proxim notifications at the next app run of any kind. New posts of revoked content are bounded by about 10 min on a running device and by W_service on an unsynced one; longer-unsynced devices post nothing; young revisions need a list under 6 h (N4, N5). Android delivered notifications time out (30 min); iOS ones have no documented auto-expiry [U] and remain until dismissed or the next run. Path S exposure is bounded by its arm horizon.
- **I2a Authoring routes:** see 9.5.

### 2.5 What is shared and what is two pipelines
This replaces every claim that the kernel is "unforked". **Shared (one implementation, test suite and golden vectors):** `proxim-detect`, the signed envelope and `proxim-pack`, the container format, geometry derivation (Rust/WASM), the renderer, consent receipts and sheet, sensitive-zone filters (R1-R9), revocation/kill switch, caps engine, OS adapter and tripwire planner, console map editor and Drive-it, simulator. **Policy data** (`mode_policies`, `policy_rules`): label key, TTLs, caps, channel allow-list, review lane, report allow-list, arm scope, selector. **Two pipelines** (separate code, vectors, policy-matrix tests): *gate* (hosted: category policy, partner caps, V x Q x R x P, pacing, diversity; owner: relation, arm scope, intent whitelist, I13), *selection* (`vqrp` vs `priority_recency`/`nearest`), *billing* (prepaid credits/CPM/share vs per-venue subscription), *moderation lane* (100% human vs 9.3a), *distribution* (public tiles vs fetch-all bundles), *reporting* (events with k-threshold vs none). Said plainly: owner mode is a second gate, selection, billing and moderation product on a shared engine, not a configuration of the first; hence it waits for the hosted pilot and a signed partner. It was tested against a third, non-resort case (8.3).

## 4. Events

### 4.1 Definitions and state machine
All events come from `proxim-detect`: `detect(state, fix, triggers, clock) -> (state', events[])`. Geometry is fixed-point integers in a per-cell local plane; side-of-line, intersection and distance use exact integer arithmetic; only the lat/lon projection uses libm, once per cell. A matcher, if built, uses + - * / and sqrt only, ties broken by (cost, edge id).

**v1 subset:** present/enter and approach/corridor enabled; exit internal only (re-arm and flap suppression, in-memory, never delivered or persisted; caps are the persisted guard after a restart); cross, pass reserved for 1.1 (M13.2); dwell (Stage 3), sequence, arm_enter, movement reserved. Reserved events are rejected by the validator and CHECK and ignored by the kernel (`UNSUPPORTED_EVENT`); each lands with golden vectors in the milestone that enables it.

Defaults (tuned in the simulator; all data):
- `acc_eff = max(reported accuracy, 10 m) x kappa` (kappa 1.0, inflated when fix residuals vs Doppler speed exceed 2x reported accuracy).
- **ENTER:** signed depth `d >= k_in x acc_eff` (1.0; 1.5 FINE) for N_in = 3 consecutive fixes spanning >= 3 s (>= 10 s if speed < 1.5 m/s); never on a fix whose accuracy circle straddles the boundary; a zone whose inradius < k_in x acc_eff is `undecidable` (ACC_TOO_COARSE). Re-arm after confirmed EXIT plus cooldown (30 min); this is detector hysteresis, distinct from the cap cooldown of N6. **OS_ENTER_CONFIRM (background wake, 5.3 Phase C):** a wake does not run the ENTER rule above or the APPROACH rule (both need several fixes). It runs a single-fix check: one verify fix with accuracy <= 100 m (N7) inside the shape or within the N7 distance tolerance; confidence INFERRED. Owner approach notifications are therefore not possible and not offered.
- **EXIT**, two paths. (i) Continuous (M1/M2): `d <= -k_out x acc_eff` for N_out = 5 fixes or >= 10 s; in-memory state held through gaps up to 120 s; 15 min without a fix resets silently to OUT; OS exit callbacks reconcile but never override a fresh confident inside fix. (ii) **Armed OS exit** (background, owner departure), valid when ALL hold: the trigger has a registered OS region; an arm record exists with `from <= now <= until` and no `fired` mark; the OS reports EXIT; one verify fix lies outside the shape by the N7 margin; the sidecar test is CLEAR (5.7); the event is within the late/far limits of N6. The OS region state is the memory of "was inside", so no Proxim visit state is read or written and any stay up to 30 d works. Confidence INFERRED; success sets the `fired` mark (one departure per arm window); an exit before `from`, after `until` or when disarmed produces nothing. "Departure" means "left the grounds while departure is armed", not "checked out". Whether Play services delivers EXIT after reboot or re-registration with INITIAL_TRIGGER_ENTER disabled is [U]; the failure is a missed, quiet departure.
- **CROSS** (1.1): oriented segment, half-width = road half-width + 4-15 m; a confident fix on one side followed within T_gap (30 s; 10 s above 10 m/s) by one on the other side with the interpolated intersection inside the half-width; per-direction cooldown 10 min. **PASS** (1.1): the track comes within `r_pass` of the point. **DWELL** (Stage 3): continuous IN >= T_dwell (>= 2 min, <= 6 h), once per visit. **APPROACH:** along-route distance enters [d_min, d_max], decreasing over >= 3 fixes, speed/bearing gates; re-armed after distance exceeds d_max x 1.2 or the anchor is passed. **ARM_ENTER/MOVEMENT** (junction, later, foreground); **SEQUENCE** (later, owner only, <= 2 steps, same sender, <= 30 min; cross-sender or long sequences are profiling and not allowed).

**No persisted visit state in v1.** Exit uses the OS region state plus the arm record. `zone_state(trigger_id, rev, state, since_bucket_15m, last_fire_bucket, ttl_expires)` is reserved for owner dwell (Stage 3). On every wake or cold start run `reconcile()`: ask the OS (iOS requestState; Android initial trigger disabled for firing), take one accurate fix, apply the detector, drop any late event older than 2x the p95 latency of the OS profile (N1). Hosted enter/cross need only in-memory counters. A device-store schema snapshot test keeps I3 literally true (only caps, preferences, consent, outbox, seen-ledger tables).

### 4.2 Reliability by location mode
**M1/M2 (1 Hz):** ENTER declared about 3-5 s after the boundary (N1, N2); tangential drive-by false enter 2.8% open, 5.2% suburban, 19.4% urban (naive 53-60%) at 0 m clearance, <= 1.4% at 50 m; stationary 25 m outside for 10 min: 0% open, 2% suburban, 34-69% urban canyon; edge flapping cut from 44-59 to 1.2-3.2 transitions per 10 min; CROSS detection 100% open, 99.6-99.7% suburban, 89.9-95.4% urban, false cross from a road 20-40 m away <= 0.2% open/suburban, 0.7-5.2% urban; PASS at r = 25 m 100% within 12 m in open sky, urban canyon 79%, r = 50 m urban 98% with 2-10% false [all MODEL]. **M3 background:** latency and distance are N1 and N2 (Android usually under 2 min, about 2-3 min average, up to 6 min stationary [V]; iOS cushion about 200 m + at least 20 s, wake about 10 s [V], delay in minutes [V-sec/U]). A ring on a public road also fires for drive-bys, so the geometry check on wake decides. EXIT has the same latency (a goodbye at the gate is unachievable, a farewell within minutes is); reports beyond `max_event_dist_m` are dropped (N3). CROSS and PASS are not available point-precisely: ring wake, bounded fix, check against the real geometry yields "arrived at the grounds", not "crossed the gate". DWELL: Android best effort, none on iOS; not promised. APPROACH ring R = cushion + v x (T_p90 + T_ready + T_lead): 4.9 km (Android) / 7.8 km (iOS) at 15 m/s, 0.45 / 0.73 km walking, 8.1 / 13.1 km highway, plus lead (N2); highway speed works only for far anchors, not on iOS.

### 4.3 Honest minimum radii and latency (what the console shows)
- **Foreground/partner-fed:** zone inradius >= k_in x acc_eff (practically 25-50 m: the detector's physical limit, not what the console accepts; nothing below the 150 m floor can be authored in v1, I18, and sub-150 m values are Stage 1.1, claimed places only); point_pass as in 3.4; gate setback >= 30-40 m from other public roads; detection 3-5 s after the crossing.
- **Background:** ring minimums N8. Promise: the notice arrives when the OS reports the entry, typically a few minutes after the boundary and sometimes later, with no guarantee. Before-the-gate delivery needs a multi-kilometre approach ring that spends a scarce slot. Wording by audience: section 7.
- **Always undecidable:** indoors, floor level, approximate-location grants, iOS Precise Location off [V]. Reason codes REDUCED_ACCURACY, ACC_TOO_COARSE, BACKGROUND_PERMISSION_MISSING, UNSUPPORTED_EVENT, HOSTED_BACKGROUND_OFF appear in `status` and the TestKit inspector.
- **Region budgets (N8):** one ring per trigger; hysteresis in the engine. Owner candidates go through the same planner (re-anchor plus leapfrog) as hosted approach rings: always-armed venues (20 garages, the N10 cap) use the whole iOS budget shared with the host, so static slots are not enough (an M11 dependency draft 1 did not state). The planner registers no ring whose whole circle lies inside sensitive buffers per the sidecar. There is no separate wake cap: a counter of non-posting wakes would be a persistent write without a post (5.3), so wake cost is bounded by one ring per trigger and by the post caps of N6, which count posts only. The ring formula replaces PLAN 5.7 step 1's density rule for every ring (critic #13). All OS numbers are data per OS release, replaced by field data at M4/M11.

### 4.4 Roads and junctions
v1: authored geometry only (road chain, buffer, bearing and speed band); direction gating reaches 94-98% correct-road in open sky [MODEL]; the console shows per-junction lead and parallel-road exposure. v1.1 pending counsel (M13.6): optional local road context per EXACT signpost (<= 30 edges within 300 m, about 0.6-1.5 KB gzip each, about 100 KB per cell) for a small HMM matcher, +2-4 points in open sky, no rescue of urban canyons (62-64%) [MODEL]; counts inside the tier budget, separate ODbL layer, CI fails above about 1.5 KB per signpost or 100 KB per cell. No global road skeleton in packs (0.27-1.28 MB gzip per dense r5 cell [M]). PLAN 5.5's "dense-urban false-fire <= 15%" becomes per profile: open sky correct-road >= 94%, suburban >= 85%, urban canyon "not promised" (reported; route intent or a confirming sequence required for exact-road claims).

## 5. Notification delivery

### 5.1 Local versus remote (decision)
**Local only.** Proxim never runs APNs or FCM and never holds a device token: remote push for location needs coordinates at the server (breaks PLAN 5.1, I2/I3) or a per-install token (breaks I5), adds latency, and fails offline in resort dead zones. Revocation is pull-based. An owner may run its own push outside Proxim; a later optional `Proxim.notifications.present(signpostId)` would let it use the same signed shell, caps and label.

**Hosted adverts as OS notifications: not built.** Apple 2.5.18, 2.5.16, 4.10, 4.5.3, 4.5.4 [V; the guidelines page showed no update date, so "dated 2026-06-08" is unconfirmed]. Apple's text says "Push Notifications", so whether 4.5.4 binds SDK-posted local notifications is open [U]. Google Play: system notifications only for integral features, ads in them are a listed violation [V-sec]. The policy matrix row hosted x any OS notification is `deny` (build flag plus signed partner config), reopened only with written pre-clearance from both stores plus counsel.

**Hosted background matching: off by default** (`partner_policies.hosted_background = off`; zero hosted rings; `HOSTED_BACKGROUND_OFF`). It can be enabled for a NAMED partner only when ALL hold: (1) the partner's core function needs background location and its purpose string and store declaration already say location is also used for nearby offers or advertising; (2) the SDK-owned sheet recorded a `background_location_for_offers` receipt; (3) the partner is not Sealed, health-category, child-directed or an owner-mode app; (4) the open store/COUNSEL question has a written answer in `docs/legal-gates.md`; (5) enablement is a signed addendum in the audit log. Evidence: the stores do not categorically ban ads from background location; Google bans requesting it solely for ads and treats extending a core-function permission to ads as a disclosure and Ads-policy matter [V-sec]; Apple 5.1.5 requires direct relevance, 5.1.2(ii) bars repurposing without further consent, 5.1.1(ii) requires complete purpose strings [V]; the FTC's proposed InMarket order alleged an SDK vendor failed to verify partners' ad-use disclosures [V-sec; finalisation [U]]. Open store/COUNSEL question: Apple defines "collect" as transmission off the device accessible longer than needed to service the request in real time [V]; the res-5 tile fetch (IP and time kept 7 days) is coarse location, so "Coarse Location" for advertising probably must be declared, and hosted background prefetch would do so off-screen. Partner-fed background fixes raise the same question; since hosted signposts render only in-app and unrendered matches are discarded at TTL, that is accepted residual risk under the same COUNSEL item.

### 5.2 Per-OS mechanics
- **Wake.** Android: `GeofencingClient` transition -> PendingIntent (FLAG_MUTABLE from Android 12) -> non-exported receiver -> Rust core -> post; `INITIAL_TRIGGER_ENTER` disabled. iOS **Path D (default):** region monitoring via `RegionBackend` (legacy CLCircularRegion now, CLMonitor behind the same interface [V]) -> relaunch with about 10 s (about 30 s with `beginBackgroundTask`) -> core -> post; requires Always. **Path S (optional, flagged):** `UNLocationNotificationTrigger` needs only When-In-Use plus notification permission [V], but content is frozen at scheduling and no fire-time check runs.
- **Limits (N8).** Android re-registers on boot, app replace, data clear, `GEOFENCE_NOT_AVAILABLE` [V]. iOS: no events under reduced accuracy; no wake if Background App Refresh is off; resumes only after first unlock [V]. **Latency:** N1 (Apple publishes none).
- **Verify on wake.** Android: the transition's triggering location when present (N7), else one bounded `getCurrentLocation`, then the sidecar test (5.7); `os_only` coarse mode only when the event carries a triggering location. **No foreground-service burst:** Google Play removes geofencing as an approved FGS use case on 2027-01-27 and points to the Geofence API [V, play-policies timeline fetched 2026-10-09; the 2026-08-26 items concern anonymous-chat apps; the policy page behind it was not read]. iOS: one `requestLocation` fix (N7), then the sidecar test; no fix: drop. The draft's "trust the OS event" option is withdrawn for notifications, because without a fix the buffer test cannot run (I16).
- **Permissions.** Android: `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` (API 33+), `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION`, `ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION` (Settings page after education UI), Play declaration with video; foreground-only or approximate-only grants mean no app-closed delivery. iOS: Path D Always (after When-In-Use); Path S When-In-Use; notification permission in both, fully granted (provisional authorization is not used, I13).
- **Posting.** Android: direct Activity PendingIntent via `setContentIntent` (no trampoline [V]); one channel "Arrival notices", IMPORTANCE_LOW, never higher from Proxim (a user-raised importance is honoured); `setTimeoutAfter(expiry)`. iOS: interruption level passive only (never active, timeSensitive, critical); thread id per venue. After tap: a verified `SignpostTap` goes to the host.

Nothing is promised for force-quit or force-stop, reboot before first unlock, or devices without Play services. One unconfirmed report says iOS 26 stopped relaunching force-quit apps from region events (forum thread, no replies) [U]; Apple DTS says region monitoring is an exception [V-sec]. Default to the legacy path; device matrix on every OS beta.

**Path S is not in the pilot's first cut:** it bypasses the Gate at fire time and cannot observe delivery. When added (flagged, after device tests): owner mode only, `repeats=false`, radius >= 200 m, passive title-only text, at most 8 requests, armed at each app run within the N4 horizon, **pre-gated at schedule time** (signature, revocation-list age per I10, and the sidecar: the whole OS region circle + 200 m outside every facility buffer + margin; at authoring the ring + the N9 cushion clear, the only guarantee possible without a fire-time check), cancelled on withdrawal or revocation poll, re-verified on tap.

### 5.2a Revocation freshness mechanism (owner notifications)
**Decision:** a fail-closed freshness window on the signed revocation list for service-class notifications (W_service, N4). The list is refreshed by any app run: foreground, the 5-minute poll while active, a partner-called `refresh()` (from the partner's own pre-arrival message), and best-effort scheduled work (N5). **Nothing at or because of a location wake touches the network, and SDK initialisation on a wake launch never starts a refresh** (a location relaunch is an app start; "poll at app start" must not apply to it). A guest not synced inside W_service gets no notification (silent drop).

Rejected: (i) periodic background refresh as the guarantee: WorkManager's minimum is 15 min and timing "depends on the constraints and on the optimizations performed by the system", it does not run in Doze, and the Rare bucket (Google's own example is a hotel app used only during a stay) allows 10 min per rolling 24 h with network "Disabled" [V]; ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION exempts only from Restricted [V]; on iOS "the system decides the best time", `earliestBeginDate` is a lower bound, and usage-pattern, Low Power Mode, Background App Refresh-off and force-quit effects are [U]; hit rates cannot be measured at scale (owner mode emits no events); kept as best effort. (ii) a non-location freshness fetch at wake: a fetch made only when the list is stale is "a stale install just woke at a ring", an arrival-timing signal (IP, time, user agent, kept 7 days) that breaks I14; revisit only at Stage 3 with OHTTP, an unkeyed cacheable object and counsel. (iii) tying validity to the booking window: Proxim cannot know bookings and long-lived per-booking bundles weaken takedown.

Worst-case takedown of revoked, still-postable content: running device 10 min (N5); Android scheduled refresh succeeding about 15 min to 4 h (Active/Working set), up to about 12 h (Frequent), about 24 h (Rare/Restricted, only with network) [MODEL reading V quotas as cadence]; iOS scheduled refresh unbounded; no refresh up to W_service; unsynced longer: nothing posts. SLA: N5.

### 5.3 Wake procedure (identical Rust for both OSes; zero network; write nothing until clear)
Order: verify without location, one fix, sensitive-zone test, presence and gate, post. The only persistent write is the cap-ledger row (plus the `fired` mark) after the post decision, so R2/R6/I6 become a testable property: **persistent writes happen if and only if a notification is posted.** The inbox is not an exception: a wake never writes an inbox row, whatever the reason; in owner mode the inbox is an in-app, foreground channel only.

**Phase A: location-free, read-only.** (1) Map the request id to a signpost in the local verified store; ignore Intent extras and userInfo. (2) Re-verify JWS, kid, rev, expiry, seq, bundle hard TTL, sidecar digest; list age <= W_service and <= `fresh_required_s` for a young revision (N4); `live_at` passed; partner kill flag and `notifications_enabled`. Delivered notifications whose signpost is on the verified revocation list are removed (a removal, not a Proxim storage write). (3) Consent receipts for `location_matching` and `local_notifications`; OS notification status; arm record present with `from <= now <= until`; class, sensitivity, intent rules. Any failure: return; nothing written, logged, queued, scheduled or fetched; no host callback exists.
**Phase B: one fix, then the sidecar test.** (4) One fix, in memory only (I3). Android: triggering location if present (N7), else one bounded `getCurrentLocation`; iOS: one `requestLocation`; no fix: return. Taking this fix to learn whether the device is in a buffer is allowed; it is discarded on IN_BUFFER or NO_DATA. Whether transient in-memory evaluation near a health facility is acceptable under Washington's geofence rule or other law is COUNSEL; Proxim cannot control the OS's own region state, location indicators or host code in a relaunch. (5) Sidecar test (5.7): CLEAR required; otherwise discard the fix and return with nothing written, queued, deferred, retried or put in the inbox. Any later fix used by the detector is tested the same way.
**Phase C: presence and gate, still read-only.** (6) `OS_ENTER_CONFIRM` (4.1: single fix, N7 tolerance, not the multi-fix ENTER rule) against the real geometry, or the armed exit of 4.1. (7) Caps and arm-record state (N6); L3 rules.
**Phase D: effects.** (8) Build content from signed fields (passive, title-only, SDK label) and post; no host code is called. (9) Write the ledger row (and `fired` mark): signpost id, rev, 15-minute bucket, no coordinates. The per-day post count (N6) is derived from these rows; there is no wake counter. No network call. Budgets N7; no FGS just to post.

### 5.4 Caps, labelling, health
- **Caps:** N6; partners may only lower them; client-trusted, reset on reinstall, backed by server anomaly checks (taps per active install). iOS has no documented auto-expiry [U]: the SDK purges expired notifications at the next run and the copy is location-neutral so residue is harmless.
- **Labelling.** The OS shows the partner app's icon and name, so a notification cannot honestly be "from" anyone else. Owner notifications put "From <verified owner>" in a fixed subtitle slot rendered from the signed sender identity and relation (`NotificationChrome`, non-editable), truthful only because O1 is enforced at authoring, signing, compile and in the kernel. No URL, no digit run of 4+, no rich text, no remote image; NFKC-normalised, bidi/zero-width stripped; alert-style emoji and a security/verify/OTP/emergency lexicon blocked; the title is renderer-built, and a compile lint plus a kernel check reject an over-long title or any body.
- **Lock-screen safety and driving: one rule.** Every location-triggered OS notification is passive (no sound, vibration, screen wake or action needing input) and title-only (N6) plus the SDK label; no body. Text is generic and location-neutral ("Welcome to Pine Ridge"): no name, room, code, health or condition content. Speed, heading and activity are not inputs. iOS cannot force preview hiding [V]. The rule does not claim to reduce distracted-driving exposure (a passive notification can still be seen; CarPlay/Android Auto behaviour is [U]); that exposure is **COUNSEL**. PLAN 5.5's "taps disabled while moving" cannot apply to an OS notification. The L4 "Turn off these alerts" action stays.
- **Health and sensitive (R6 absolute).** health_adjacent, health_facility and prohibited_anchor signposts never produce an OS notification, lock-screen exposure, App Clip or ephemeral notification in any mode, including a facility messaging its own patients. Sealed or health-category apps emit no location-triggered notifications. Inside any sensitive buffer every notification surface is dropped for all classes. Hospital, clinic, mental/reproductive/substance health, shelters, worship, schools, childcare, immigration, correctional, union and military venues are excluded from owner notifications; user-initiated flows (QR, "I have arrived") remain. COUNSEL: Washington's statute names notifications [V-sec].

### 5.5 Consent model
- Purposes (separate, default-off): `location_matching`, `aggregate_reporting`, `nearby_health_events` (unchanged); `local_notifications` (agreement, in the partner's UI, to location-triggered OS notifications, class=service; distinct from `location_matching` and the OS permission, all three required); `background_location_for_triggers` (opt-in to arrival assistance, with the SDK receipt; the OS prompt and Google's prominent disclosure remain the partner's); `background_location_for_offers` (hosted, reserved, off, not built until a partner qualifies under 5.1); `promotional_notifications` (reserved, off, not built).
- The SDK-owned `ProximConsentSheet` and device-bound receipt are mandatory for `local_notifications`, `background_location_for_triggers` and `background_location_for_offers`, not just health (FTC InMarket, Gravy, Kochava point at SDK vendors' consent verification [V-sec]). The sheet states what location is used, that it stays on the device, what triggers (enter; exit for armed departure), the caps and how to withdraw; decline as prominent as accept; withdrawal is one tap and clears pending and delivered notifications, outbox, and unregisters OS regions. Owner line: "Proxim does not tell this venue's app when you arrive, but your phone may start the venue's app in the background when you enter the area, and the venue's app may be able to tell. The venue's own app may use location in other ways, described in its own privacy notice." The Play declaration, video, Data safety and App Privacy answers must state what the system really does and not present a promotional feature as core.
- Apple 5.1.2(i), 4.5.4: location and notifications are never required to use the app; the partner keeps an always-available in-app "Arrival guide". The SDK never requests Always or `ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION` (PLAN 5.3, 5.7, 7 stay literally true). Only the partner-wide CMP master switch may gate SDK network.

### 5.6 SDK API (experimental in v1; every symbol is a promise)
Eight symbols, no coordinates, identifiers or user attributes: `Proxim.notifications.configure(presenter)`; `.status` (needsOsPermission, needsLocationPermission(level), reducedAccuracy, paused(consent), armed(count), stale(since:), degraded); `.requestOsAuthorization(provisional: false)` (partner-called only); `.arm(signpostIds:from:until:)` (`from` defaults to now; at most 30 d; arrival armed from before check-in, departure only from check-out day); `.disarmAll()`; `.taps` (verified `SignpostTap`, only after the user opens a notification); `Proxim.consent.set(.localNotifications(.service), granted:)`; `Proxim.notifications.refresh()` (call from your own background wake or pre-arrival push; fetches only non-location metadata). Kotlin mirrors it. **There is no veto and no callback on enter, exit, cross, dwell, approach, a Gate decision or a suppression** (I14c); `presenter` runs only for in-app rendering while visible; `stale(since:)` derives from list age. Notification code ships only in optional artifacts (`sdk-background` / `ProximBackground` or a small `ProximNotifications` product); base artifacts declare neither POST_NOTIFICATIONS nor background location (I11). Owner mode emits no events.

### 5.7 Sensitive-zone data in owner bundles (the venue sidecar)
An owner-only app makes zero tile fetches (I8), so it cannot read the registry from tile packs. Every owner bundle revision therefore carries its own signed sidecar `sz`; without a valid one the kernel refuses to post. Its points are the non-confidential registry data public tile packs already carry, so it adds no disclosure beyond I15.

**Posting envelope.** P(V) is where any of a venue's notification signposts could post: the OS ring; shape + the 100 m verify tolerance (N7) for arrival (owner approach is not offered, 4.1); shape + min(`max_event_dist_m`, 5 km) for departure (a notification posts where the device is when the OS finally reports, not where the ring is). Coverage C(V) = P(V) offset by `buffer_m` + `margin_m` (N9), from the shape outline. Departure distance dominates size; the console shows it, and a departure signpost in a dense area is refused rather than silently under-protected.
**Contents.** Header (`registry_version`, `built_at`, per-class `buffer_m`, `margin_m`); coverage polygon (<= 64 outward-rounded vertices, venue-local fixed-point plane); list `{class_id u8, x i32, y i32}` of every NON-confidential facility within `buffer_m(class) + margin_m` of P(V); no names, addresses, NPIs or fine kind labels. An empty list is valid only inside a present, signed sidecar with coverage; absent is not empty. Each signpost's envelope carries `sz_sha256`, so stripping or swapping the sidecar invalidates it.
**Confidential kinds are never shipped, not even as coarse cells.** (1) The compiler and console reject any venue whose C(V), computed with the confidential buffer, intersects a barred H3 res-7 cover cell, with the single refusal message ("not available at this location"), equalised timing and the 9.5 rate limits; the initial confidential set (DV shelters plus anything the registry-governance ADR marks address-confidential) comes from the per-kind publication ADR of PLAN-REVIEW #4. (2) On every registry update a job re-tests all live owner venues; a hit suspends the venue's notification signposts through the revocation list and tells the owner generically; suspensions are applied on a fixed, jittered batch cycle with a message naming only "registry update", so timing does not reveal a new confidential site nearby. (3) C(V) already includes buffer and margin, so the device needs no confidential data.
**Kernel rule (fail closed).** `sz_check(fix)` returns CLEAR, IN_BUFFER or NO_DATA. NO_DATA: no sidecar, digest mismatch, parse error, sidecar older than the bundle hard TTL, `registry_version` below the signed minimum, or the fix's accuracy circle not wholly inside coverage. IN_BUFFER: any facility within `buffer_m(class)` + fix accuracy. Only CLEAR continues; the others end the wake with nothing written, queued, deferred, retried or put in the inbox. Exact integer squared distance. The owner's foreground in-app card is not a notification surface and is unaffected.
**Limits.** Above the N9 facility count, notification signposts are refused for that venue ("not available at this location": the same message, timing and response shape as every other refusal; the in-app alternative is offered on every refusal, whatever the cause); the pilot-site rule (9.6) is stricter. The sidecar is a snapshot valid for the bundle hard TTL; a facility opening after the build is invisible until the next bundle (shortened by rebuild-on-registry-change, which relies on 5.2a). Sizes: N10; the densities behind them are assumptions the M5 registry import replaces.

## 6. The "beneficial" gate (layered)

A notification should be shown only if the app or the AI has decided it is beneficial to the user. Resolution: five layers; none builds a user profile; no runtime AI on user data inside Proxim; no decision callback into the owner's app.

| Layer | What it decides | May use | May NOT use |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 Intent whitelist (signed) | Which channels are eligible. Notification-eligible: wayfinding, arrival_service, departure_service, event_info, transactional, safety_info. Promotion only in owner mode with `promotional_notifications` consent (later). Advertisement: never | Declared intent, mode, class, place-derived sensitivity | Sender-declared sensitivity |
| L2 Authoring-time review (human + Claude advisory) | Copy specific, timely, useful at that place, free of urgency tricks and condition implications, lock-screen safe, not promotion in disguise | Sender content as data (structured output, no tools, fail closed on outage) | User data; model output never edits content |
| L3 Deterministic on-device rules | Place-bound (radius <= 2 km), time-bound or per-visit; non-redundant (suppress if the owner's app is foreground, already delivered this visit, or stale per `max_event_age_s`/`max_event_dist_m`); caps (N6). No speed logic: every OS notification is passive and title-only | Location, heading, time, partner context, the owner's arm list on device | Inferred traits, history beyond the cap ledger, ad IDs |
| L4 User control | Per-intent toggles, "Turn off these alerts", OS channel mute, dismiss/hide/report | The user's explicit actions, stored locally | Positive interest learning (P4 off; negative-only suppression allowed) |
| L5 Owner-side arming (declarative, v2.0) | Which of the owner's signposts are armed on this device and until when, decided before the visit from the owner's booking state; disarmed on check-in, cancellation or opt-out. Nothing runs at decision time | The owner's own data about its customer | Any callback, stream or return value from the decision path. Not in hosted mode |

**No host veto (decision).** `hostVeto` is removed from v2.0 and not planned for Stage 3. The oracle is the call itself: it fires only after presence is confirmed and the Gate passed, so identity-holding host code learns "this user is inside the venue now" whatever the payload; dropping the distance bucket only reduces precision, and opt-in plus disclosure would make the oracle declared but still present while the sheet's "location stays on the device" would mislead. The goal (the app decides using its own customer data) is met by declarative arming. An owner app holding Always can still run its own location logic; Proxim only declines to be the conduit (I14c). DPA owner clause: the venue shall not use any SDK output (taps, status, launch reason, delivered-notification state) to infer or record presence, arrival or departure, except for an action the user themselves started [COUNSEL].

**Hosted partners.** No decision-time callback in hosted mode either, for the real reason: arbitrary identity-bearing partner code in the decision path would let a hosted signpost depend on who the user is (the billboard principle) and Proxim could not audit it. Place-category inference is not the reason: hosted headless partners already see which signposts matched through the `Proxim.signposts` SignpostDecision stream and the visible slot (PLAN 7), and M1/M2 partners already hold the location. That inference is accepted, pre-existing and controlled by the partner DPA (decisions used only to render, cap, reconcile billing and measure reach; no profiling, health inference or joining to a user record; Sealed partners write decisions to no user-linked store; COUNSEL); it is in the data-flow inventory as an on-device flow that never reaches Proxim. Hosted suppression flags have no return value; a suppressed slot is indistinguishable from no match; disabled for Sealed or health partners.

Aggregate feedback (mute and opt-out rate) drives server-side auto-pause and owner warnings. "Why this?" copy states the real logic (place, time, app context, topics you chose) and never says "AI chose this for you". Per-guest AI personalisation, if wanted, lives inside the owner's app on the owner's data.

## 7. Worked example: a resort, drive-in, notification, guide, and what stays in the owner's backend

"Pine Ridge Resort" has its own Android and iOS app, run by the same organisation (O1). A guest with a booking drives in along a 3 km private access road to a main gate, then reception. Pine Ridge is rural and passes the 9.6 site test (for a city hotel next to a pharmacy this walkthrough ends at step 1).

**Console setup (resort staff)**
1. The org completes tier >= 1, claims the venue with a physical-address proof (postcard/PIN letter), binds its app signing identity, and is checked against the venue exclusion list (covered-entity provider, treatment centre, senior-care campus, R4 category). A human assigns `venue_exposure_class` = A. The venue declares any on-site or adjoining medical, first-aid, pharmacy, medical-spa or senior-care facility; the 9.6 site test runs before the wizard continues (an unavailable site hears only "not available at this location"). First venue reviewed manually.
2. Wizard: search "Pine Ridge Resort"; use the Overture resort polygon or draw the grounds (Class A, at most 50 vertices); the shape must include the access road, or drive-in arrival is mostly dropped (N3, N7). The public main entrance as mapped may anchor (a custom shape at the 150 m floor in v1).
3. Rules: Arrival = enter(grounds, inflated to the background minimum), intent arrival_service, local notification (no inbox fallback at a wake, 5.3), one per arm window (N6), expiry 30 min. No Approaching ring in owner mode: a wake has one fix and cannot satisfy the approach rule (4.1). Departure = exit(grounds), passive, **armed only from check-out day**, dropped when late or far (N6); the console says "Departure means the guest left the grounds while departure is armed, not that they checked out." Gate welcome = cross(in) at the main entrance, in-app, foreground/partner-fed only (1.1 capability).
4. Copy: title "Welcome to Pine Ridge" (<= 7 words, <= 40 characters), no body; the label "From Pine Ridge Resort" is renderer-built. No names, room numbers or codes; the linter blocks digits, "password", "wifi", "code", "PIN", tokens in URLs.
5. Experience: a guide (Welcome; Reception and parking pin at the public main entrance; guest Wi-Fi network name only; Dining; Activities; Help: the front-desk number on the public website and the local emergency number). No staff names, personal numbers, gate/door procedures or codes. "Open my reservation" deep-links into the resort app.
6. "Drive it": foreground gate-level, fires 3-5 s after the gate; background grounds-level, a qualitative warning with no distance figure and "On a 3 km access road the notice may arrive after the guest reaches reception." Labelled simulated; no probability or minutes; "not promised after force-quit"; "share of your venue where notices would be dropped" (zero here).
7. Submit: the audience warning (9.4) and checkbox; automated checks (domain-bound links, Web Risk, secret/operational-detail lint, AI benefit rubric); the reviewer ticks all nine checklist items. A new signpost takes the human lane with a 24 h hold (`live_at`, N4). The worker compiles an **owner bundle set** (signed ES256, `aud` = the resort app, `audience_scope = app_bundle`, Class A polygon, the sidecar `sz` with `sz_sha256` in every envelope, N10 sizes). The rings are NOT in public tile packs.

**Device**
8. Booking created in the resort's backend. The resort app (not Proxim) calls `arm` for `arrival` over [check-in - 1 d, check-in + 1 d] and `departure` over [check-out day start, check-out + 1 d]. Arm records are encrypted on the device, never uploaded; arming causes no request.
8b. Pre-arrival: the resort sends its normal pre-arrival message by its own channel (Proxim is not involved). Opening the app is the primary path; the partner may also call `refresh()` (coalesced, I14b) and should do so for all installs on a fixed schedule rather than per guest, because per-guest timing near a booking is visible to Proxim. Either refreshes the list and bundles and shows `status = armed`. If the app is neither opened nor refreshed inside the freshness window before arrival (N4), no arrival notification is posted and the guest uses the in-app arrival guide: a stated integration requirement and contract term.
9. Every install fetches the owner manifest and all bundles on its normal schedule, before and independently of `arm()` (2.3a), with the publishable key and no location; the app verifies the JWS and caches. The guest opted into "Arrival assistance" in the resort's UI, which triggered the SDK receipt, the OS notification prompt and the Android background-location flow (resort-owned prompts, Play declaration and video; G-O1).
10. The planner registers the grounds ring unless the sidecar shows the whole ring inside buffers.
11. The guest drives in. The OS reports the ring crossing minutes late (N1, N2). The core runs 5.3. A: signature and sidecar digest, list age within W_service (within 6 h if the resort changed notification text, geometry or a link domain in the last 72 h), `live_at`, consent, arm window. B: one fix, sidecar CLEAR. C: inside, caps. D: one passive, title-only notification with subtitle "From Pine Ridge Resort" and one ledger row. Any failure, including a stale list, posts and writes nothing. If notifications are off or denied, nothing is posted and nothing is written (no inbox row); the in-app arrival guide is the only fallback.
    **A guest who never opened the app after booking:** the ring still wakes the app (if the OS regions persist), Phase A finds the list older than W_service and returns silently: no notification, no inbox row, no fallback request, no host callback. The same holds for a booking made beyond the bundle hard TTL (N4). When the guest next opens the app the list refreshes (`status` shows `stale(since)` until then) and the always-available in-app arrival guide (Apple 5.1.2(i)) is the path. Unopened for 3, 8 and 21 days: 3 d posts only if a refresh ran inside the window; 8 d and 21 d never post; tested with and without a successful scheduled refresh (11.8).
12. Tap: a direct PendingIntent opens the host with signpost_id, rev and event type only; the SDK re-verifies; the guide renders from cache (works offline); "Open my reservation" navigates inside the resort app.
13. The resort app authenticates the guest with its own session and loads room, folio and door instructions from its backend: the first guest-specific moment, entirely outside Proxim.
14. Leaving: on check-out day the OS reports the guests leaving the grounds (minutes late, N1); one verify fix confirms they are outside by the N7 margin; the sidecar test is CLEAR; one passive title-only notification posts, dropped if late or far (N6; at driving speed a share of iOS reports exceeds the distance, N3). Dinner on day 2 is not armed and posts nothing. This works after a 3-night stay because no stored visit state is needed.

**What Proxim sees.** Sees: the console content; that app X fetched manifest, bundles and config (IP, time, key; no cell or location; identical in content for every install; a partner-called `refresh()` can time a fetch near a booking, a declared residual, I14b); revocation polls and scheduled-refresh fetches of unkeyed global metadata at times unrelated to location; flat billing. Does not see: any coordinate or route; a fetch caused by an arrival (none exists); that a trigger fired or a notification was posted or tapped; who the guest is; the booking; the arm list; any push token. The resort sees what its own app collects; the SDK exposes no callback on arrival or exit; the SDK tells the venue's app of an arrival only if the user opens a notification (`taps`) or sees an in-app card, but the app may infer one from OS-visible facts in its own process (a location relaunch, its own delivered notifications and sandbox; I14c residual); its own location permission is first-party processing for its privacy notice (COUNSEL on roles). Lock-screen text is visible to bystanders; Android geofencing runs through Google Play services, so geofence definitions may be visible to Google [U].

**Honest promise copy (no latency, distance or hit-rate number in contracts or sender UI; vendor quotes in developer docs excepted, 2.6)**
- Contract (all platforms): "Proxim triggers the arrival notice when the guest's phone operating system reports that the guest has entered the area. Operating systems deliver these reports with a delay that varies: typically within a few minutes, sometimes later, occasionally not at all. Timing is not guaranteed, and Proxim commits to no latency, distance or hit rate. On short access roads the notice may arrive after the guest has reached the building. Nothing is promised if the app was force-quit, the phone was restarted and not yet unlocked, location, notification or background-refresh permissions or battery-saving modes prevent it, the guest has not opened the app within the freshness window stated in the order form (taken from `promises.yaml`), or the guest is in an area where notices are suppressed."
- Developer docs, Android: "Google documents geofence alerts as usually under 2 minutes, about 2-3 minutes on average when Android's background location limits apply, and up to 6 minutes if the device has been stationary. Proxim then takes one verification fix and posts. At 54 km/h each minute is 0.9 km."
- Developer docs, iOS: "Apple reports a region boundary only after the device has moved a system-defined distance past it (about 200 m for testing) and stayed there at least 20 seconds, wakes the app for about 10 seconds, and does not wake the app if Background App Refresh is off. Apple publishes no typical delay; plan for several minutes. Region monitoring needs Precise Location."
- Sender console (no probabilities or minutes; shown when the model's internal threshold is exceeded, which is not displayed): "Background arrival notices are delayed by the phone's operating system by a few minutes; at driving speed that is kilometres. If guests reach reception soon after your boundary, the notice may arrive after them."

**Cheaper alternatives to offer the resort** (returned at G-O0 as the alternatives note): the gate or check-in system fires the resort's own push with Proxim supplying only the guide; an Apple Wallet pass with up to 10 relevant locations [V-sec]; QR or NFC at the gate to an App Clip or web guide for first-time visitors (who cannot receive a notification from an app they have not installed; Google Play Instant has ended [V-sec]).

### 8.2 Staged roadmap

## 9. Abuse controls

### 9.1 Narrow geometry (stalking a home, workplace or clinic door)
Risk: a tier-0 sender draws 10-30 m on a victim's driveway and reads counts, billing lines or landing-page hits as a presence oracle. In v1 no sub-150 m geometry exists for any sender (I18, tested at M9); these controls land with Stage 1.1 (M13.1) and owner mode.
- **Geometry classes.** G0 (any sender): radius >= 150 m. G1 micro (building, gate, entrance, point_pass): only with a level-2 place claim over a public-access, non-residential feature, outside sensitive buffers, with footprint at or above the accuracy gate. G2 road/junction: through-traffic classes only; cul-de-sacs, service roads, driveways barred. G1/G2 get flight-level reporting only. Floors live in `policy_rules`, enforced three times (DB, compiler, kernel).
- **Claim ladder.** Level 0 none. Level 1: domain DNS + KYB + callback to an independently listed number. Level 2 (G1 and owner mode): Level 1 + physical-address proof, a two-person rule for residential-adjacent features, per-feature binding, annual expiry, non-transferable, audit-chain logging, and an objection channel that pauses the signpost immediately.
- Triggers are part of the immutable signed revision (PLAN section 4 hole closed; pgTAP). **Reporting as an oracle:** G1/G2 flight-level only, threshold on the NOISY value, no `partner_app` dimension in sender-facing releases, no per-day counts, per-sender privacy budget across overlapping geometries, flat-fee billing, reach from static priors; PLAN-REVIEW #6's "exact counts for non-health" excludes G1/G2; differencing and padding attacks are our own analysis [U], demonstrated in the simulator first. Residual: a tap reveals IP and time to the verified owner of a claimed place; no URL macros.
- **Authoring oracle.** Generic "geometry not permitted here" for causes involving confidential kinds, equalised timing, rate limits (N11, 9.5), no confidential perimeters in packs or bundles, place search excludes residential/private records for non-claimants; the near-identical-rejection alert works at r5 resolution and compares finer geometry only in memory against the same org's drafts. **Venue envelope check:** the compiler computes C(V) (5.7) and rejects a venue touching a confidential barred cell, re-run on every registry update; near-identical rejections from one account or IP alert. **Declared residual:** probing the wizard's "Available / Not available here" answer across many points can map barred cells. Mitigations: one message and timing for every cause (density included), the site test only after the level-2 address claim, the N11 rate limits plus a per-org cap on distinct-location tests; not eliminated (COUNSEL and the registry-governance ADR).
- Limits N10; NaN/antimeridian/self-intersection rejected; parser fuzzing; no bulk address upload. Exit, dwell and sequence reveal a visit: owner mode only, never on health_facility or prohibited anchors, suppressed inside any standoff with nothing persisted (R7).

### 9.2 Notifications (spam, phishing, spoofing, residue)
Kernel-only posting at wake time after every hard filter; a lint fails the build on `UNLocationNotificationTrigger`, `UNCalendarNotificationTrigger` or alarm-scheduled content in any third-party path. Structured template, renderer-built title and label, Unicode and lexicon rules (5.4); phishing corpus (security alert, verify account, package held, emergency, bank, hospital results, faked "Sponsored", faked "Public information", bidi, homoglyphs), zero false-approves. Caps, cooldowns, hysteresis (N6); server anomaly detection of taps per active install with suspension and clawback. Residue: location-neutral text, expiry (5.4), barred sensitive classes, no sound.
**Revocation.** Scopes signpost+rev, signpost, sender_org, kid; triggered by org admins, staff, report thresholds, the crawler and Web Risk. Bounds in N5; delivered notifications are purged at each poll and wake. Measured at M5 (simulated device, 100 revocations), M12 (real-device drill, 20 runs per OS) and M18; the SLA is published only from M12 numbers.

### 9.3 The owner-mode loophole (advertisers posing as owners, health bypass)
- Relation O1 (2.3) by DB constraint `content_mode = 'owner' => sender_org_id = partner_org_id`, the signer, the compiler and the kernel; a hosted key cannot set `content_mode` or `relation`; `owner_mode_enabled` is staff-only after a documented core arrival feature and a store-listing check; no partner-facing binding API.
- Closed `intent` taxonomy (draft 1 called it `purpose`) and label key in the signed revision; a promotion cannot be downgraded to service after approval; mixed content is promotion. URLs https on the verified owner domain only; no custom schemes, macros or off-domain redirects; owner app routes limited to an allowlist in the signed partner config; SSRF-safe fetcher with rebinding tests. Sensitivity class derives from the place registry, never the sender; R1-R6 apply; venue exclusion list with a human step; residential estates: guests, visitors, contractors only, resident exit off by default.
- No per-impression billing or rev share removes the incentive to disguise ads. **Linked-org check** at org creation (shared payment fingerprint, bank account, admin email domain, phone, registered address, domain registrant, IP/ASN, device); **brand-mention rule** (owner copy naming an entity other than the sender's verified names goes to a human; a third-party brand is rejected); per-partner anomaly counts (sender orgs, link domains, taps per install); the N11 unreviewed-org limits; contract bars selling relations; apps whose main function is monitoring another person are screened out.
- **Rogue-sender suite** (extends PLAN M7; lands with the feature; before owner mode exists RS-OWN-01/02 run on the hosted path and assert no owner or notification route exists; removing a case needs an ADR; fresh red-team at M18). One advertiser-posing-as-owner corpus, at least 60 human-labelled items, zero false-approve, fail closed on model outage. **RS-OWN-01** mall-tenant promotion (200 tenant orgs, some shells; "Welcome to Brand X. Tap for today's offers."; direct write of `content_mode = owner`): zero OS notifications for any sender other than the partner org in 1,000 runs (NOTIF_THIRD_PARTY), the write fails for every role, shells flagged. **RS-OWN-02** an agency's thin wrapper app becomes partner and sender and sells "welcome" notifications: zero approved; the portal cannot enable owner mode. **RS-OWN-03** estate perimeter ("the side gate is unattended after 22:00; keypad 4821", gate transects, APK extraction, false claim): Class B byte-grep finds no fixture gate coordinate; guide rejected on checklist items 2, 3, 6. **RS-ATO-01** credential theft ("Account security notice. Tap to confirm your booking", lookalike domain, leaked API key, lapsed-domain takeover, email-reset takeover, waiting out the hold): each ends in rejection, hold, auto-pause or freeze; nothing renders before `live_at`; a device with a 7 h old list drops a young revision. **RS-VETO-01** a partner registers every output and logs calls: zero calls before a tap in 1,000 synthetic drives; no exposed type has fields lat, lon, distance, bucket, ring, cell. A launch-reason and delivered-notification probe in the host is recorded as a documented, not prevented, channel (I14c).

### 9.3a Owner-lane revision review, account security, bounded blast radius
Owner notification content is evergreen ("Welcome to X"), so friction on changing it costs almost nothing; that asymmetry is the main defence.
**Lanes** (by server-side structured diff of the immutable revision, never an LLM; unknown class = human):
- **Human, 24 h hold:** notification title, label, intent, purpose; geometry, events, schedule, expiry or arm window beyond the approved bound; delivery channels; experience type; images/assets; any link, button, deeplink or map pin in a guide; new signposts; partner route allowlist. Link-domain changes (new registrable domain, web URL domain, deeplink route outside the allowlist, phone, payment, tel/sms) also get DNS re-proof, lookalike check and domain age shown.
- **Auto, 1 h hold with owner alert and one-click revert:** text or heading edits to an existing approved guide on a live signpost, only if there is no link/button/deeplink/image/pin/phone change, lints are clean, no payment, credential, urgency, security or promotion lexicon is introduced (currency, percent, "offer", "sale", "free", "verify", "account", "deposit", "login"), N11 limits hold, the Claude advisory (advisory only; fails closed to human) sees no risk-class change, and the org has no account-risk flag. Rollback to the last human-approved revision is automatic and immediate. Post-hoc sampling applies to the auto lane only and is 100% (daily digest) at pilot scale.
The reviewer sees the diff, DNS proof, domain age, lookalike score and account-risk flags (new device in 72 h, recent recovery, impossible travel, failed-MFA burst); reviewer != author; hardware key; aal2 on Approve; audit chain; target one business day, inside the hold.
**Cool-off.** `live_at` per N4 is signed (I17); the signer refuses earlier values reading the database independently; the device refuses a revision before `live_at`. No self-serve waiver; a staff expedite exists only for genuine safety_info after a callback to the independently listed number with a second staff approver. Alerts at submit, approve and go-live go to the org security contact (changing it takes a 72 h hold plus callback) and all admins, with a "this was not me" link that cancels the revision and freezes the org (unfreeze needs a staff callback).
**Account takeover controls (owner-lane orgs).** MFA (aal2) for all members; passkey/WebAuthn for anyone who can submit or approve notification-affecting revisions; SMS never a factor (Supabase WebAuthn support [U]; fall back to TOTP plus an external IdP); step-up, session limits and recovery freeze per N11; device-bound sessions where supported ([V-sec]), else step-up on IP/ASN/user-agent change; no email-only reset; sender API keys read status and upload drafts only (revoke allowed); with two or more admins a second owner-side approver is required; new signposts are never armed implicitly; an org freeze is one revocation entry.
**Tap targets and recrawl.** A notification opens a `guide` or an allowlisted app deeplink only; `web` is reachable from the inbox or an in-app card. Recrawl per N11: https, valid TLS, final URL on the same registrable domain, no new password/payment input or off-origin form; SSRF-safe fetcher from two egress networks with a mobile user agent; failure auto-pauses by revocation. Residual: cloaked pages.
**Bound on a stolen credential.** The attacker needs a live session AND a fresh passkey assertion; API keys cannot submit; a human reads the change and it cannot go live before submit + 24 h while the real owner is alerted three times; only revisions of already-armed signposts reach devices; text cannot carry a URL, digit run, security lexicon or rich text; the tap goes to a reviewed guide. After go-live a device posts it only inside the ring, once per trigger per arm window and at most 2 per day per app (N6), and only with a list not older than 6 h. Residual: a reviewer fooled by a plausible revision from a real credential holder, a device offline up to 6 h after downloading it, iOS lock-screen residue.

### 9.4 Confidential-content leakage
**Premise.** The publishable key is not a secret, tile packs are public, and an owner bundle can be pulled by anyone with the app. Nothing here makes a bundle secret; it limits what is in it. `audience_scope` takes only `public_tiles` or `app_bundle`; the words private, secret, protected, restricted, hidden, secure, internal and members-only are banned for bundle content in console strings and docs (CI string lint; "private access road" as physical prose is fine).
**Console warning (exact copy; key `audience.warning`; at venue creation, as a banner in every guide editor, and as a checkbox at save-for-review and submit).**
Title: **Anyone with your app can read this**
Body: "Everything you publish here is downloaded into your app. Anyone who installs your app, or extracts your app's key, can read it without logging in. Treat it like a public web page. Do not include door, gate or Wi-Fi codes, staff names, personal phone numbers, security or emergency procedures, or anything you would not print on your public website. Keep guest-specific and sensitive details behind your own login. Proxim shows a short generic message and opens your app."
Checkbox: "I confirm this contains nothing I would not publish on my public website."
**Contents.** A human assigns `venue_exposure_class` (default B when unsure; a sender cannot lower it). **Class A** (public-access: resort, hotel, museum, park, parking operator, event ground): grounds polygon, ring, the public main entrance as in public map data, reception/parking/public-facility pins (N10). **Class B** (estate, HOA, residential, restricted access): one circle (N10); never gate, transect, point_pass, junction or corridor shapes; never pins at gates, guard houses, entrances or service routes; a notices guide with no access, gate, security or after-hours content; the authoring boundary stays in the server database (org members and staff) and is never compiled into a bundle or pack. **Never in any bundle:** codes, passwords, staff or resident names, personal phone numbers, booking data, emergency or security procedures, camera/patrol/fence detail, QR payloads, tokens in URLs.
**Human review checklist (all nine ticked before Approve is enabled; version stored in `moderation_reviews`).** (1) Public-website test: everything could appear on the verified public website. (2) No credentials or codes (door, gate, safe, lockbox, Wi-Fi password, alarm, PIN, booking number, token in a URL). (3) No gate or entrance procedure (who opens it, staffing, after-hours or unattended access, tailgating, key/fob handling). (4) No named individuals or personal contacts; only role-based numbers on the verified domain. (5) No security, emergency or incident procedure beyond "call the local emergency number" and the published main line. (6) No perimeter or layout detail beyond a public map (fences, unmonitored entrances, service routes, back of house). (7) Class B: the preview shows only the ring. (8) Images: no signage, whiteboards, screens, badges, keypads, plates or faces; OCR/QR scan clean. (9) Not promotion: no price, offer, discount or third-party brand.
**Linters (necessary, not sufficient).** Secret lint (digit runs, "password", "wifi password", "code", "PIN", key-like tokens, secrets in query strings, QR-like payloads) plus an operational-detail lexicon (gate, barrier, guard, patrol, after hours, unattended, unlocked, combination, keypad, key box, lockbox, alarm, staff only, back of house, override, on-call, night manager, "ask <first name>"); any hit forces the human lane. CI byte-grep of every compiled pack and bundle for fixture secrets and Class B perimeter vertices; zero false-approves on a 100-item corpus with at least 30 operational-detail items. A semantic leak can pass every linter; checklist, attestation and contract allocate responsibility.
**Patterns and leak response.** Pattern A (default): teaser (<= 90 chars) plus opaque `ref`; the owner's backend authorises by session, never by knowing the ref. Pattern B (later): confidential geometry served by the owner's origin with a Proxim-signed manifest. Sealed pages only if the key is released by the owner's backend after login (a key shipped in the app protects nothing). Content-addressed means integrity, not retention: on a leak report revoke the revision, delete the object, purge the CDN (N11, drilled at M12) and tell the owner to rotate the secret; downloaded copies cannot be recalled and the contract says so. Per-key/per-IP rate limits and a many-ASNs alert detect extraction; they are not protection. Proxim does not open gates or hold access credentials (Apple 5.1.5).

### 9.5 Coordinate-bearing authoring endpoints (I2a)
Sender-authoring routes carry coordinates by definition. (1) A route is coordinate-bearing only if tagged `x-proxim-coords: true` in the OpenAPI source; the lint allowlist is generated from the tag and an untagged route accepting lat/lng-shaped or address fields fails CI. Covered: `POST /v1/ref/at`, `GET /v1/ref/search` (address text is a coordinate in disguise), `GET /v1/ref/features/{gid}`, `POST /v1/triggers:preview`, trigger/zone create and update. (2) Coordinates in a POST body, never a URL; `Cache-Control: no-store`, `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`. (3) Bodies excluded from application logs, OpenTelemetry and Sentry; the scrubber drops lat/lng-shaped and address fields; access logs hold route template, status and latency bucket only. (4) No retention beyond the sender's own RLS-protected draft; lookups write nothing; rejections only as `authoring_rejections(org_id, user_id, cell_r5, reason_code, at)` for 30 days. (5) Rate limits per N11; 429 with `Retry-After` and no cause; equalised timing on "not permitted here". (6) Drive-it is client-side; the browser never POSTs a route. (7) Tile Range requests reveal the viewed area to Proxim only; request logs 7 days. Residual: Vercel and Supabase platform logs may capture request metadata outside our control [U]; checked at M0.

### 9.6 Sensitive-buffer coverage for owner mode, measured before it is promised
**Why.** I16 drops every notification, for every class, inside any sensitive buffer (N9; the registry includes hospitals, clinics, pharmacies), refuses triggers whose geometry plus hysteresis touches a buffer, and persists nothing there. Pharmacies, clinics and campus medical centres are dense in cities and on campuses, so many urban and campus venues cannot be served (PLAN-REVIEW #5; owner mode is stricter). Nothing in the use-case map is promised until measured; I16 is not narrowed (decision 6).
**Tool.** `tools/coverage owner-buffer` (Rust geometry plus PostGIS; offline; public data only): inputs the pinned `sensitive_places` snapshot (NPPES NPI-2, OSM, Overture places, sender-declared and surveyed facilities), the `jurisdiction_rules` buffer, and a venue polygon or metro box.
