## 1. What changes (one page, cut v1)

**What a business can do in v1**
1. Type or paste its copy into a **fact sheet** and 1 to 6 wordings of one message (a **family**). Optionally press one button, 'Suggest wordings': ONE AI call drafts up to 5 more wordings from the typed fact sheet and the sender's own copy. 'Upload your campaign' in v1 means paste or type existing copy and duplicate a past campaign. AI is an untrusted author: its text passes the same lint, sender acceptance, attestation, human review and signature as typed text.
2. A family sits inside ONE signpost revision: reviewed once, signed once, one set of caps, dismiss, hide and report. At most 6 wordings (4 for new, unverified senders; 3 for health classes, typed only).
3. Lock the facts. Price, offer, dates, hours, phone, address, code and landing page are typed, attested facts. Wordings reference them only by placeholder. A deterministic check, not the AI, enforces it.
4. Choose where and when, and a cadence preset such as 'every weekday'.
5. Publish: an admin or owner clicks Submit (the internal approval and the advertiser attestation); a person at Proxim reviews the family; the signer signs; the pack head advances.
6. Share (three things, section 6): PUBLISH, TEAM (members and expiring viewer guests), RESULTS (a read-only snapshot link of already-released weekly cells).
7. See honest numbers: billed views per campaign-week, settled spend, family-level taps, saves, dismiss and hide with a stated margin or 'not enough data', and redemptions the sender enters against the family's one code. **Per-wording results do not exist in v1**, and the console says so.

**What changes on the phone.** The device picks which approved wording to show with a pure, integer-only rule over its own day-level ledger and public inputs (5.1). Nothing about the choice leaves the phone. While driving it shows only the glanceable, with no tap target.

**What we promise and do not promise**
- Promise: 'eligible every weekday when a partner app is open on your road' and 'never one of the last three wordings in the same app' (families of 4 or more).
- Do not promise: 'delivered every weekday' (the commuter needs a partner app open, consent, a free slot, pacing and caps), 'no ad fatigue' (a wording returns after about N exposures), 'a different wording every day across two apps' (best effort for pilot partners with designed lanes, never contract copy, N-37, N-38), per-person frequency or reach, which wording won, or conversions from drivers.

**What the plan gains and what it loosens (section 9.2 has the full table)**

## 3. Campaign creation

### 3.1 One editor, two entry paths
- **Typed path (always available; M7a).** Fact sheet, then 1 to 6 wordings typed or pasted, with live counters and checks. Duplicate a past campaign; Proxim-written starter templates (at most 20, token slots, no user content). The console says: 'Your ad is text plus a small icon. Pictures and flyer import come later.'
- **AI path (per-org gate G-AI; M7b).** The sender picks category, offer type, tone, goal and locale from closed lists and may type a base wording and up to 3 short highlights. ONE server call drafts up to 5 more wordings (one `angle` each), at most one repair call. The sender must accept each draft (`accepted_by`). No autopilot, no chat, no wizard.
- **Not in v1 (G-1.1S):** URL import, pasted-text or flyer/menu/PDF extraction, the free 'describe it' box, free-text targeting parse, CSV bulk, pictures and brand kit.

### 3.2 Facts (claim-locking; never cut)
Facts are family-level, typed, attested and evidenced: `brand_name, price, original_price, discount_pct, offer_start, offer_end, promo_code, phone, address_line, hours, exit_or_road_ref, url`. At most 12 per revision. There is no free-text fact type.
1. **Prompt side (I25).** The prompt is built only by the typed serializer `LlmPromptV1`. The model sees: Proxim instruction text; closed-list category, offer type, tone, goal, angle, locale; placeholder NAMES and TYPES; and the sender's base wording and up to 3 highlights after placeholder substitution, passed through a reject-not-strip validator (digit, number word, currency, date, URL, email, `@`, brace, control/format/tag characters, non-allowlisted script, phone and address patterns, street-suffix words). It never sees the brand name, any fact value, targeting, pages, documents or customer data, as far as the filters can tell: the filtering is best-effort, not a guarantee. Names, nicknames, town or landmark names and brand variants typed into the sender's own wording are not on the reject list and WOULD reach the vendor; this residual is part of the disclosed processor flow (COUNSEL 6). The pre-call check therefore also rejects any base wording or highlight that contains a fuzzy match (NFKC, case-fold, de-space, tokens of 4 or more characters) of any fact value, the brand name or the org name, and the AI button says: 'The wording you type is sent to our AI provider; do not include personal names.' Egress canaries include spelling, spacing and abbreviation variants. The model writes `{brand}`, `{price}`, `{offer}`, `{hours}`, `{address}`, `{phone}`, `{road_ref}` placeholders (the model-writable subset of the one closed enum PlaceholderV1 in I25; the serializer, lint and Zod schema are generated from it); the server substitutes attested values in ONE pass (values cannot contain braces) and enforces lengths after the call. The signed text is final text; the SDK stays a dumb renderer. The JSON schema sent to the API contains only fixed enums.
2. **Fact-span lint on the expanded text of EVERY wording**, typed or AI: any number, currency, percent, date, time, phone, URL, address-like span or number word ('half off') must resolve to an attested fact. v1 text uses a closed English charset (Basic Latin, Latin-1 letters, listed punctuation, a closed emoji set), NFKC-normalised, confusable-folded and de-spaced; digits appear only through placeholders. A naive ASCII lint passes 14 of 20 hand-written evasions; allowlist plus lexicon passes 0 of 20 [M, 20 hand cases only] (N-46). The 200-string evasion corpus and the lint **ship with M7a**, not M7b.
3. **Qualitative-claim lexicon** (superlatives, '#1', 'guaranteed', 'free' without an offer fact, comparatives, medical and financial claims, testimonials, ratings, urgency tricks, second-person statements about habits or location history such as 'back again?'): blocked or forced to the reviewer with a flag.
3a. **V10 (L-GEO).** Signed text contains no literal distance or ETA (a number or number word with a length or time unit), no relative direction or position ('ahead', 'next exit', 'on your right', 'coming up'), no manoeuvre instruction ('turn', 'merge', 'pull over'). Signed text is static, so '1.2 km ahead' is false at most points of a corridor. Where place matters use a static fact (`exit_or_road_ref`, `address_line`). Word lists are policy data reviewed by counsel; AI output that trips V10 is dropped, typed text is blocked with a plain message.
4. **Dated claims bind validity.** `valid_until = min(flight_end, end of offer_end day in campaign time zone)`, signed.
- Residual: a fact-free semantic claim ('kid friendly', 'open late') can pass the lint. Lexicon, the reviewer's assertion list and the human reviewer are the controls. Provenance (`source`, `ai_run_id`, model id, template hash, fact-sheet hash) is stored.

### 3.3 Creative fields
- Per wording: `headline` (40 characters), `body` (90), `glanceable` (2 to 7 words and at most 40 characters of expanded text; mandatory, the only text a driver sees), `icon_id` (closed SDK set, at most 3 icons compatible with the category; not credited with variation), `angle` (closed enum). Word counting: whitespace split, numerals count as words.
- Glanceable placeholders allowed: `{brand}` (short form, at most 16 characters), `{price}`, `{offer}`, `{road_ref}`. `{address}`, `{phone}`, `{hours}`, `{url}`, `{code}` are not (nothing to act on while driving).
- **Family-level, never per wording:** landing URL, **`cta_kind`** (closed list: directions, call, url, save, book, order, menu, offer), the **one promo code**, sensitivity class, sender identity, geometry, cadence. A wording may differ only in wording, icon and glanceable (billboard principle, 9.2).
- Angles in v1: benefit, convenience, price (needs a price fact), location_cue (only with a static road or address fact, never relative position), question, plain_info. Excluded: social_proof, scarcity, comparative, superlative, time_cue.
- Text plus icon only. No pictures, logo upload, brand kit or image pipeline (G-1.1S; Appendix A).
- **Attestation at submit:** 'I have the right to use this text and every fact is true', recorded by user and time. Any fact change needs re-attestation.

### 3.5 AI pipeline (server only) and injection defence
1. **One drafting call, no extraction.** No tools, no network, no memory, stateless per tenant, inline text only. The only attacker-controlled text reaching the model is the sender's own screened wording; the worst injection outcome is a bad DRAFT, which meets the same lint, sender acceptance, attestation, human review and signature.
2. **Output handling.** Structured outputs do not enforce length bounds and a refusal or `max_tokens` stop can return non-conforming text [V per the critic's fetch]. Check `stop_reason`, parse, enforce lengths in Zod after the call, at most one repair call, still invalid means dropped; text is never truncated. Placeholders must be a subset of the fact sheet's keys.
3. **Diversity gate (V6).** Headline pairs AND glanceable pairs each need d >= 0.35, d = min(normalised Levenshtein, 1 - bigram Dice) on NFKC lower-cased punctuation-stripped expanded text. Symmetric and portable, with golden vectors (difflib is neither: asymmetric on 136 of 300 hand pairs [M]). Measured on 10 hand-written families by one author [M, proxy weak]: headline pairs 0 of 150 fail (min 0.47); glanceable pairs 22 of 150 fail (14.7%) (N-47); V10 hits 8 of 60 glanceables; mean usable distinct lines per family 3.9 (distribution {1:1, 3:3, 4:2, 5:3, 6:1}); AI proxy 59 of 60 lines pass. So unconstrained copy yields about 4 driver lines, not 6; the compiler never counts a wording that fails the glanceable gate as a rotation member, and the console shows 'Drivers will see N different lines'. Threshold calibrated on one small corpus [U]; re-tune before G-AI.
4. **No fetch for AI.** The SSRF-safe fetcher serves the landing-page crawl (4.2c) only.
5. **Request allowlist (I2a, I25).** No coordinate, geometry, place/road/address search text or targeting choice in any LLM request; the console's outbound HTTP client is host-allowlisted. Targeting suggestion is deterministic. Prompt and response bodies are excluded from logs, OpenTelemetry and Sentry.
6. **No advisory review LLM in v1.** An LLM may add friction, never remove a human requirement (I25 (d)).
7. **Eval gate.** About 30 briefs (including adversarial ones), 15 injection fixtures and 60 poisoned-output vectors gate any model, prompt or schema change; the 200-string lint-evasion corpus runs in CI from M7a (N-45). Egress canaries prove no fact value reaches a captured request body. A small corpus is a regression tripwire, not a safety proof.

## 4. Variants: the creative family model

### 4.1 Shape
A signpost revision holds `creatives[0..n-1]` (index 0 is the base). One signature covers the envelope and the digest of every declared creative (4.3). Decisions:
- **One revision, not N signposts and not N signatures.** Dismiss (7-day rest), hide and report only mean something if all wordings share one signpost identity; caps (2 per day, 5 per week per signpost; 3 per day per sender) must not multiply by N. One review item, one audit record.
- **Per-variant signatures rejected** (23 to 30 percent more bytes at N of 4 to 8 [MODEL], and unnecessary).
- **Concurrent revisions per slot rejected.** Changing a wording is a new immutable revision superseding the previous one; delta review keeps it cheap.
- **Creative digest and `vid`.** `d_i = SHA-256('PROXIM-CREATIVE-V2' 0x00 || canonical_creative_i)`. `vid_i` is the first 4 bytes of `d_i`: the key of the on-device ledger and of variant-scope revocation, **never** evidence that a body is authentic (I10, I17). The compiler rejects a `vid` collision within a revision and the device re-checks, failing closed. **Events carry no wording index:** the server never learns which wording was shown.
- **Caps (policy data [U], N-36).** Wire format n <= 8; v1 N <= 6 (base plus up to 5 AI wordings); tier-0 senders 4 (the pilot cohort's tier is NOT yet decided: the reviewer table in 4.2 assumes tier 0 at up to 4 wordings, while section 2 and 5.4 headline numbers use 6; if the pilot is domain-verified, rerun the load model with the 6-wording mix at S=15, and if tier 0, the cadence numbers are the N=4 ones, e.g. return gap 5.6 days); health classes 3, manual only (no AI); editor default 6. Claims at most 12. Review queue and pack bytes grow linearly, gains beyond 6 are slow [MODEL].
- **Not sender-configurable:** rotation order, weights, sequencing, exposure-history targeting.

### 4.2 Review model: what a human approves, what is automated
| Layer | Who | What |

### 4.2a Inheritance key and re-approval triggers
`inherit_key = SHA-256('proxim/inherit/v1', full creative digest, facts_sha256, exact landing URL set, ctx_sha256)`; `ctx_sha256` covers category, sensitivity class, sender org and domain binding, audience scope, schedule and cadence class, partner categories, locale and geometry class. ### 4.2c Landing checks, capacity and flood control
- **Landing snapshot and crawl.** Snapshot at approval (final URL and chain, text hash, fact spans, outbound domains, form fields, screenshot); static-fetch crawl from two egress networks; HARD drift (off-origin redirect, downgrade, new payment or password field, Web Risk hit) auto-revokes, SOFT drift flags a human. About 5,600 fetches a day at 100 senders; a k-a-day crawl detects a swap after 24/(k+1) h on average, so a once-daily crawl catches a 3 h swap 12.5% of the time (N-55). **Declared residuals:** crawler-aware cloaking and JavaScript-conditional redirects (no headless rendering or residential egress in v1).
- **Hold and response.** A report hold is automatic and reversible (REPORT-HOLD, 9.2) and works at any hour; a human responds within 4 business hours, never auto-released. First human response clock time: median 17 h, p90 55 h, max 68 h; 76 to 79% of holds start outside business hours. Promise: 'held automatically, reviewed within 4 business hours', no weekend human response (N-50, N-56).
- **Staffing.** One reviewer covers about 55 senders (P50) or about 40 (P90); two cover about 110. At least 2 trained reviewers before the first external sender: one reviewer leaves the promise at risk 30 business days a year, two 3.5, three 0.4 (11.5% absence) [MODEL].
- **Flood control.** Invite-only pilot of at most 15 senders (planned cohort 8 from about 20 candidates). Tier-0 lane takes at most 25% of reviewer capacity. A review flood costs 3.3 / 16.7 / 66.7 / 333 reviewer-hours at 10 / 50 / 200 / 1,000 shell orgs (N-51); pre-human gate (lint, crawl, near-duplicate of a rejected family) and strikes limit it.
- **Measure, then re-run.** M7a records real minutes per creative; M13 re-runs the load model before the intake limit is raised.

### 4.3 Signature and immutability
- **Verification path (normative; fixed by `golden_v2.json`).** `signed_msg = 'PROXIM-ENV-V2' 0x00 || u16(len(core)) || core || u8(n) || d_0 .. d_(n-1)`; the signature (ES256, 64 B) covers the core envelope plus the **full 32-byte digest of every declared creative** (n <= 8 wire, <= 6 in v1). The wire omits the digests of shipped bodies, which the device recomputes; each unshipped creative would carry its digest. The device parses strictly, recomputes digests of shipped bodies, requires pairwise-distinct `vid` prefixes, verifies the signature over the rebuilt message and renders only shipped bodies. **Any parse, duplicate-vid or signature failure drops the whole entry.**
- `ship_mask` (1 B) is unsigned and withdraw-only; creative 0 always ships; the signed manifest tier hash covers it. **The v1 compiler is all-or-none** (the build fails rather than ship `n_shipped < n_declared`); the v1 verifier already accepts masks so G-1.1S can enable partial shipping without an SDK release.
- **Merkle root rejected** (mean saving 8 / 8 / 16 / 20 / 26 B at N = 4..8; extra rules for four ports) (N-33).
- **Evidence.** A second preimage of a 4-byte `vid` was found in 16.7 s on 4 cores (2.96e8 hashes; expected 2^32 about 242 s) [M]. 702 swap cases are rejected by the signature alone; 20,000 mutations produced 0 accepted-but-different entries; Python and an independent Rust implementation agree on the golden file (20 selection traces, 487 ops, 19 envelope entries including the vid-collision fixture) (N-34, N-35). Golden vectors must pass in both before M2 exits.
- The signer signs only if an independent DB read shows an approved review at the envelope and at every creative digest, and a deterministic fact-containment re-run inside the signer succeeds.
- `expiry` is at most `min(valid_until, 14 days for orgs younger than 90 days or 30 days otherwise)`. Renewal re-signs identical content into an append-only table and requires a passing landing crawl. Revisions stay immutable.
- No dynamic creative: no macros, on-device generation or per-user text. Rotation reads only the public inputs in 5.1 and can change which approved wording shows, never what it says.

### 4.4 Pack-size budget and N cap
- Family bytes with the 65 B signature and all bodies shipped: **F = 515 + 110N**; general form F(N,k) = 515 + 110k + 32(N-k) bytes for k bodies shipped of N declared (N-30). Planning figure of 110 B per body is deliberately pessimistic; measured 40 to 52 B gzip on 60 hand-written creatives [M]; real AI text may compress worse [U].
- **Capacity of a tier-0 pack (350,000 B) [MODEL arithmetic, N-31]:** N = 1 / 2 / 4 / 6 / 8 gives F = 625 / 735 / 955 / 1,175 / 1,395 B and 560 / 476 / 366 / 297 / 250 families. 'Top 500' was an N = 1 number. Signatures cost 500 x 65 B = 32.5 kB = 9.3% of the pack (12.3% at the 86 B text form).

- **Compiler warning:** when gcd(n, D) > 1 for D scheduled days a week the editor warns (5.5); the editor defaults to 6 wordings (N-40).

## 5. On-device rotation and fatigue control

### 5.1 Algorithm (`select_variant`, step 7b in `proxim-decide`, pure and integer-only)
Policy H replaces the per-install least-recently-shown tie-break of the first draft (finding S5). The rotation reads no install salt, clock or random source (I26, 9.2).

Inputs: signed family (declared digests d_0..d_(n-1), `rot_days` = 7-bit weekday mask written by the compiler from the signpost's schedule, bit 0 = Monday, 0 reads as all days), signpost id, availability mask A (shipped, locale, not variant-revoked), the partner app's public lane (`rot_lane`, `rot_lanes`, signed per-partner-app config), local day number (days since 1970-01-05, a Monday; device-local calendar date) and the ledger.
```
vid_i = u32(d_i[0..4]);  key_i = u32(SHA-256('PROXIM-ROT-V2' 0x00 'ord' || signpost_id || d_i)[0..4]);  pos_i = rank of (key_i, i)
phi   = u32(SHA-256('PROXIM-ROT-V2' 0x00 'phase' || signpost_id)[0..4])
sched_index(day, mask) = (day div 7) * popcount(mask) + popcount(mask & ((1 << (day mod 7)) - 1))
cn[v] = 0 if no row or day - last_day > 90, else (count * DECAY[day - last_day]) >> 16, and 0 if < 13   # evaluated at READ so ports agree
K     = min(3, |A| - 1);  ban = K most recent vids in family_state.recent (empty if family_state older than 90 days);  cand = A minus ban
elig  = { i in cand : cn[i] <= min(cn over cand) + 256 }
t     = (sched_index(day, rot_days) + phi + (lane * n) / lanes) mod n
pick  = the i in elig minimising (has_live_row(i), (pos_i - t) mod n, i)     # wordings without a live row first, then nearest slot at or after today's
new_count(i) = min(65535, max(cn[i] + 256, 1 + max cn over the other members of A))   # fixed at decision time, written on commit
commit (5.3): count = new_count, last_day = day, recent = last 4 vids; skipped inside any sensitive buffer (R2/I6)
dismiss: count = min(65535, cn + 512)
```
- **Public lane config.** Lanes are public, signed per-partner-app configuration (a constant of the app, not of the person). Pilot partners get explicit lanes; two co-installed pilot partners must differ (config lint); there is no implicit lane 0. Non-pilot apps use a hashed lane, which behaves like the first draft (5.4).
- **Hard property (test, not statistic).** Inside one install the pick is never the last shown wording (|A| >= 2) and never one of the last 3 (|A| >= 4), whatever the open pattern (N-38).
- With one app opened on every scheduled day this is the round-robin cycle. The date prior matters only when opens are irregular or two apps share a phone. The `new_count` rule stops a freshly added wording from being shown 2-3 times before old ones return (repeats in the first 6 showings after a 3-of-6 refresh: 3.0 for per-install LRS without the rule, 0.49 for LRS with the rule, and 1.0 for policy H as specified here, which is also 1.0 after a single added wording where LRS with the rule gives 0.0 [MODEL, rotation2/scen2_out.txt as read by the verifier]).
- Driving mode shows only the glanceable. The inbox row is bound to the vid chosen at queue time; opening the inbox does not advance rotation. `golden_v2.json` is the normative vector file (5.2, 13).

### 5.2 Ledger
`variant_ledger(family_id, vid, count, last_day)` and `family_state(family_id, recent[<=4 vids], last_day)`; whole days. A row reads as absent when `day - last_day > 90` or its decayed count is below 13 (evaluated at read; physical deletion is lazy and unobservable); `family_state` reads as empty after 90 days. At most 2,000 rows with LRU eviction by smallest `last_day`, then smallest vid. That limit is a dead-letter net (live rows <= 244 per app under the caps) and must never be set below N-1 per family, which breaks rotation (N-41). Excluded from backup, encrypted, wiped by consent withdrawal and `eraseAllLocalData`, listed in `privacyReport`, never written inside a sensitive buffer, readable only by the selector (reader allowlist in `check-invariants`). I3 is clarified in 9.2 (variant ledger and family_state named).

### 5.3 Interplay with caps, cadence, emission and user controls
- **Cap unit is the family** (the signpost id). Per signpost 2 per day and 5 per week, per sender 3 per day, per app 1 per 30 minutes and 4 per day are applied before wording choice.
- **Signed `cadence`** (stricter-only): `max_per_day` 1 or 2, `max_per_week` at most 5, `min_gap_h`. Console preset "every weekday" = 1 a day, 5 a week. With the defaults of 2 and 5 a commuter who passes twice a day gets an ad on only 61 percent of weekdays; with 1 a day it is 100 percent at full open rate, 70 versus 91 percent at 70 percent open, 61 versus 64 percent at 40 percent open [MODEL]. A true 7-day "daily" cannot be met under the 5-per-week ceiling.
- **Emission, `markShown` and caps (S12).** A decision is *emitted* when the SDK hands a wording to the host (partner-fed callback or slot result). The kernel keeps one memory-only pending decision per signpost `{decision_id, vid, new_count, t_emit}`:
  1. Asking again within W_EMIT = 60 s returns the same decision; no new emission is counted.
  2. `markShown` inside the window commits caps, cadence and ledger and, if the viewable rule holds (7.1), queues one `view` event.
  3. If no `markShown` arrives, window expiry commits caps, cadence and ledger exactly as in 2 (source `emit`) and queues NO event.
  4. `markShown` after expiry but within L = 10 min queues the `view` event if viewable and changes nothing else (no second charge). Later calls are ignored.
  5. Billing, reports and partner payout stay on `markShown` only; a host that never calls it is never billed and its partner earns nothing, but its emissions still consume caps.
  6. The sensitive-buffer rule applies to the step-3 commit; a process killed inside the window loses the pending decision.
  7. TestKit counts emit-commits and fails the scripted walk when more than 5 percent of decisions expire unconfirmed; partner-fed integrations must pass that sandbox gate (at least 95 percent confirmed) to go live.
  Headless host, two passes a day, caps 1/day and 5/week: first draft 9.78 emissions a week, 100 percent of days over cap, 100 percent repeats; with this rule 4.89, 0 and 0 (N-39) [MODEL; W_EMIT and L are [U]]. Caps now count committed decisions (tightened, 9.2).
- **Decay** (PLAN 5.6 step 7) is a per-wording ranking weight with a 14-day half-life (integer table, 91 entries). Across families it is only a ranking factor, never a gate and never a `p_serve` multiplier. Read literally as a gate it gives about 5.3 / 4.8 / 4.0 exposures in 60 days against 44 / 30.8 / 17.6 opened commute days [MODEL]. Labelled LOOSENED in 9.2; caps carry frequency control.
- **User controls survive rotation.** Dismiss (7 days; the whole family rests) and hide (permanent for that signpost) key on the family; report stays permanent for that sender on that device (PLAN 5.6 step 5, unchanged); all are checked before wording choice. The selector can never re-enable a suppressed family. A dismissed wording goes to the back of the queue and returns after at least N-1 other showings (6 at N=6, measured), not "about two".
- **Pacing** is unchanged and independent of wording; a thinned day is a budget effect and the report says so. Hosted background stays off.

### 5.5 Honest limits and what is deferred
1. **Promise only what is tested.** Promise: "eligible every weekday when a partner app is open on your road" and "never one of the last three wordings in the same app" (families of 4 or more). Cross-app variety is best effort for designed-lane pilot partners only and is never contract copy (N-38). Do not promise "a different wording every day across two apps" or "no ad fatigue".
2. Proxim cannot verify per-person frequency or per-wording evenness in production; v1 events carry no wording index (7.3). Evenness is shown by the proxim-sim scenario set (`commuter_60d` one app and two app lanes, `two_app_same_lane` which must fail the lane lint, `headless_host`, `periodic_visitors`, `weekday_binding`, `reinstall`, `dismiss_rest`, `gap_purge`, `lru_bound`, plus two scenarios still to be written at M3: `revision_refresh` (new-revision insertion, the 3-of-6 and add-one cases of 5.1) and `autocorrelated_opens` (Markov plus holiday opens for policy H)), golden vectors on every target, a 60-day fake-clock run of the real SDK builds compared with the Rust reference, a 20-exposure on-device test, and a staff-device local decision log through `exportLocalData()` (never uploaded).
3. Two apps, a reinstall, a new device, or a headless host defeat or distort rotation. Cross-app capping and unique reach need an identifier (I5) and are not offered. Cross-app repeat avoidance is reduced, not prevented: both apps compute the wording of the day from public inputs. With designed lanes and daily opens in both, the two apps never repeat each other's wording within 5 showings of a 6-wording family; at 70 percent opens the gain is about half; at 40 percent it is small.
4. The wording of a given day is predictable from the public pack to anyone with the same lane. It reveals nothing about a person.
5. Rotation fixes wording repetition only, not frequency fatigue or banner blindness; the cap of 5 a week bounds frequency. The console recommends a refresh revision about every 4 weeks.
6. A driver sees only the glanceable (7 words, no tap target). Wording variety for drivers lives in those words; icons are not credited. Measured on hand-written copy a family yields a mean of 3.9 usable distinct driver lines of 6 (N-47), so the editor reports the distinct count and the compiler never counts a creative failing the glanceable gate as a rotation member.
7. Variant quality varies; the lint catches near-duplicates, not bland copy.
8. No server overlay, weights or bandit in v1 (reserved fields stay inert; all-or-none shipping in v1, 4.4).

### 6.3 Read-only snapshot link (the only link type in v1)
- **Content.** A frozen set of already-released C1 cells for at most 10 signposts (under the same campaign-week display rule as 7.4), the org display name and campaign names. Never member emails, comments, geometry, coordinates, drafts or raw events; non-general classes show family-level cells only. A re-fetch returns identical numbers because releases are stored once.
- **Token.** 256 bits, only its hash stored, carried in the URL fragment. The page's same-origin script POSTs it in the body to one endpoint and renders the returned cells with `textContent`. **There is no cookie of any kind** (a reload needs the fragment again).
- **Origin and headers.** Separate registrable domain (never a subdomain of the console); CSP `default-src 'none'; script-src 'self'; connect-src 'self'; style-src 'self'; frame-ancestors 'none'`; `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`; `X-Robots-Tag: noindex`; `Cache-Control: no-store`; no CORS headers. A GET without a fragment (unfurlers, crawlers) returns a static shell with no data. Unknown, expired and revoked tokens return the same 404 with equalised timing. Served by a `share_reader` DB role that can execute one SECURITY DEFINER function.
- **Lifetime.** Default expiry 7 days, maximum 30, at most 5 active per org, instant revoke; view count and last-view day only, no IP. Creator removal suspends their links.
- Blast radius of a leaked link: the sender's own noisy weekly counts for the link's lifetime. Preview links, passcodes, view caps, sender secret keys and templates are G-1.1S or later. Tests: RS-SNAP-01/02, RS-TEAM-01/02 (13).

### 7.2 What is not measurable (stated in console and docs)
Store visits and footfall; view-through conversion; cross-app or person-level reach, frequency and de-duplication; per-person journeys; incrementality; hour of day, position along the corridor, lead distance or cell; which wording was shown or worked (v1 events carry no wording index); deliveries that did not happen. Reach and frequency are not shown (exposure bits are not in v1, 7.8).

### 7.3 Events (additive to PLAN 6.2 item 3; closed enums, padded to a fixed size)
`{event_id, signpost_id, rev, type: view | tap | save | dismiss | hide, slot, UTC day, sdk/os version buckets, attested, simulated}`.
- **Stored row.** No token, device id, session id, hour, cell, IP, user id, version index or creative id. `var`, `cta`, `exp`, `rpt` are CHECK-disabled; no field is derived from the device's exposure history or rotation state (I24, 9.2). No events at all for `health_facility` (I7).
- **Ingest token (PLAN 6.3 Phase 1, unchanged).** A stateless HMAC capability issued after an attestation check, **multi-use within one UTC day**, capped at C_tok = 20 events per token per day; issuance capped at I_tok = 1 token per attestation key per UTC day. Counters (`ingest_token_counters(token_hash, day, n)`, keyed by attestation-key hash for issuance) hold no event reference and are purged at 72 h (N-19, N-20). Ingest validates the MAC, counts and discards the token. The first draft's single-use nonce, `ingest_nonces` table and "closes B2" claim are withdrawn.
- **Declared residual (policy only until Phase 2).** Within a UTC day, events sharing a token or IP are linkable by anyone who sees request logs, and the issuer can link a token to an attestation. In v1 the linkable content is only (signpost, type, day) tuples. Phase 2 (OHTTP plus blind tokens, RFC 9578 / RFC 9458 [V-sec; search summaries only]) remains the hard gate for health traffic and GA, and a precondition for any history-derived field (Appendix A).
- **Device.** One event per request with a random 1 to 24 h delay; outbox TTL 7 days; the server rejects `day` older than 8 days (N-22).
- **Why 7 days (N-25).** An app opened 0.3 times a day delivers 85.6 percent of events with TTL 7 d (94.2 percent at 10 d, 98.2 percent at 14 d), 62.1 percent at 0.15 a day, if every device behaves identically. With lognormal cadence spread (sigma 0.5 / 1.0 / 1.5) the event-weighted share at median 0.3 is 88.6 / 93.3 / 96.9 percent [MODEL]. Low-cadence apps (parking, EV) still under-report; this is reported per partner as "reporting loss" (7.7), is not grossed up into billing and is never presented as a defect of a sender's campaign.

### 7.4 Pipeline
1. `POST /v1/events`: closed schema (unknown field 422), duplicate `event_id` rejected, attestation and rate checks, IVT flags; insert-only role; raw purge at 14 days (60 under a dispute hold).
2. Nightly exact rollup into internal `event_aggregates_daily`. **Never readable by sender roles.** Feeds pacing and the internal budget stop, fraud, partner payout, reconciliation and the pack builder.
3. **Release job**, the only path to sender numbers (I22). The weekly cube C1 for week W is released once, at least 8 full UTC days after W ends (N-22); the event set is closed by then. No ad-hoc queries, no other releases. A cell exists for every (signpost, week) in which the signpost was live for at least one UTC day, whether or not events occurred (existence depends only on public status). Each cell is stored once as a SIGNED integer `noisy_value` (may be negative; no clamp in storage). Noise is a deterministic function of the cell key and a KMS-held secret, so a re-run returns the same value; the integer discrete-Laplace sampler must be exactly symmetric (no one-sided truncation) because billing relies on zero-mean noise [integer-sampler sufficiency U]. The DISPLAY value is max(0, noisy_value), shown only if at least T (N-2); otherwise "under T". Sender roles read a display view; signed values under T are internal. **Campaign-week display rule (closes the differencing path from the billing total):** per-signpost view cells of a campaign-week are shown only if EVERY view cell of that campaign-week is at least T; otherwise the sender sees the campaign-week total and the 'minimum applied' bit only, so the total can never be subtracted against displayed cells to recover a hidden signed value. When all cells are shown they sum exactly to billed_views (each is at least T, so S is at least 20). The rule applies to the Signposts tab, the snapshot link and every export. Test: for any campaign, the displayed cells and the total never determine the signed value of an undisplayed cell (property test with a decoy-busy-signpost negative control).
4. **Weekly billing close** from the stored signed rows (7.7).
5. **Weekly reconciliation** (7.7).
- **Freshness is a schedule, not a count.** Week W appears at least 8 full UTC days after it ends ("week W appears on <date>"). Nothing is added to a released cell and nothing is re-released. There is no "late arrivals" row. The console says in words that apps opened rarely can under-report and that billing is on what arrived.

### 7.5 Release design (v1: ONE cube, noise plus a display threshold)
| Cube | Cell | Metric: per-device weekly cap, scale b, sd |
|---|---|---|
| C1 weekly | (signpost, week) | view: cap 5, b=5, sd 7.06 (billing basis, 7.7). tap: cap 5, b=2.5, sd 3.51. save: cap 5, b=5, sd 7.06. dismiss: cap 1, b=1, sd 1.36. hide: cap 1, b=1, sd 1.36 |

Standard deviations are [MODEL arithmetic] for the discrete Laplace (N-3, N-4). Weekly tap, save, dismiss and hide cells are internal release objects; the sender sees flight sums and the rates in 7.9.
- **Threshold.** T = 20 on the noisy view value, for DISPLAY only; it never changes a charge (N-2, 7.7). Health_adjacent: family-level C1 only, T = 100. Health_facility: none. G1/G2: one flight-total cell (addendum 9.1). The 150 m geometry floor is kept.
- **What this is.** Noise plus a display threshold. It is not differential privacy; no privacy budget is claimed, computed or enforced; there is no epsilon column and no `privacy_budget_ledger`. Internal arithmetic only: one device at its weekly cap can move the cells by up to 5 views, 5 taps, 5 saves, 1 dismiss and 1 hide; sum(cap/b) = 6 per cube-week per app, 36 over a 6-week flight, 72 with two apps (N-18); caps are client-enforced. Collusion between senders and overlapping signposts are not budgeted (known gap).
- **Observer accuracy [MODEL] (N-17).** For an observer who knows a cell's baseline and asks whether one persistent commuter (+5 views a week) passes: raw release 0.698 / 0.858 / 0.987 after 1 / 6 / 26 weeks. With the 20-view floor: baseline 0 gives 0.507 / 0.540 / 0.627; baseline 10 gives 0.552 / 0.675 / 0.810; baseline 15 gives 0.642 / 0.787 / 0.950; baseline 20 or more gives 0.698 / 0.859 / 0.987. Protection is strong for one-off events and lone commuters in near-empty cells and weak for regular commuters in busy cells. Regression limit: accuracy at baseline 0, 26 weeks must not exceed 0.65, measured for an observer who reads the displayed cells AND the campaign-week total with a busy decoy signpost in the same campaign (this observer reaches the raw 0.698 / 0.858 / 0.987 if the 7.4 display rule is absent [MODEL, verifier recompute observer2.py]). The floor rows above are claimed only for single-signpost campaigns and for campaign-weeks hidden by the 7.4 rule. Declared residual: a sender who knows the other signposts' true counts (for example from its own landing logs) can subtract them from the total and recover a noisier value of a quiet cell (sd 7.06 x sqrt(m) views for m signposts), with accuracy between the floor and raw rows; N-17 has not been re-run for that observer [U].
- **Release policy, not a budget.** `release_policy` is a static allowlist enforced by CHECK (v1: the five C1 metrics at period `week`). Adding any cube, period or metric needs an ADR and privacy-reviewer sign-off. Labelled REMOVED (BUDGET-LEDGER, 9.2): it removes a control the first draft added, and the replacement is a review gate, not a computed bound.
- **Cost to small senders [MODEL; inputs U].** The weekly view cell is displayed about 93 percent of the time at a true 29 views (p10) and about 100 percent at the median 114.75. 5.6 percent of pilot senders have under 20 true weekly views and 10.6 percent under 29 (N-12). Daily cells (C2), per-version cells (C3) and cumulative releases are not in v1 (Appendix A).
- **Open for the privacy reviewer (not adopted):** releasing taps, saves, dismiss and hide in disjoint 4-week cells would raise the share of senders with a readable CTR (+/-50 percent) from 11.3 to 22.5 percent (N-61).
- Anything in a public pack derived from reports is computed from released cells and quantised. Model numbers never appear in sender copy; `check-promises` also bans "epsilon", "differential privacy" and "anonymous".

### 7.6 Conversion mechanism: one promo code per family
- **One code per family per revision**, typed by the sender as a claim-locked fact (letters and digits, at most 12 characters), identical for every device and wording (I21, 9.2). Shown as a "Show code" chip on the card and in "Seen on the road" (never on the driving glanceable). Unique in the sender namespace, bound to the signed revision, never reused, expires with the revision. **No code and no conversion reporting on `health_adjacent` and `health_facility`.** `cta_kind` is a family field.
- **Visibility floor.** A stricter-only pack flag hides the chip while the signpost's exact trailing-14-day viewable count is below 100 (fallback if the flag is not ready at M3: no code chip in the pilot). About 22.5 percent of pilot senders (weekly viewable under 50) lose the chip, about 3.2 percent of expected redemptions (N-64) [MODEL].
- **Why per-delivery, per-device, single-use and per-tap values are banned (I21).** A unique code redeemed at a till joins a place and a day to a POS record or loyalty ID, an identifier and presence oracle, a data type Apple would count as tracking [V as tagged in the privacy report]. Enforced by schema (`conversion_codes.scope = 'family'`), compiler lint (no macros), no SDK substitution path and a CI property test (N simulated devices emit identical URL and code sets). Tap-bound nonces are not in v1 and not recommended.
- **Landing URL.** The signed URL opened exactly as compiled, host equal to the verified sender domain, https, no shorteners, no macros, no hop through Proxim. Static campaign-level parameters `utm_source=proxim&utm_medium=signpost&utm_campaign=<slug>`; no per-version `utm_content`; health classes get none.
- **Entry (v1).** Console form (day, count) or CSV (date, code, count, optional value in cents). Extra columns rejected; a cell matching an email or phone pattern rejects the whole file; a cell with a formula prefix is rejected; the file is parsed in memory and not kept (hash and row counts only). Row, size, date and count limits apply. Redemptions never affect billing, ranking, pacing, refunds or holds (I23) and are not evidence in billing disputes.
- **Console copy (no model numbers).** "Redemptions are the counts you enter. The code is the same for everyone who sees this campaign, so we can count redemptions but cannot tell which wording prompted them, whether the person saw your ad, or who they are. A code can be shared, so some redemptions come from people who never saw it. Redemptions never change what you are billed. Read small counts as proof the channel works, not as a rate."
- **Attribution** window 7 days default, 30 maximum; conversions bucket by the sender's local date while deliveries are UTC, so compare weekly; implausible counts raise a flag that is staff-only: it is never rendered to the sender and never reflected in the accept or reject behaviour of the form or CSV (only fixed public limits apply), and a test asserts the redemption endpoints return identical responses for any count.
- **Residual (I-6, accepted).** A named loyalty-card holder who redeems the code tells the sender the person probably saw the signpost or got the code from someone who did; the code is also readable from the public pack. At the median pilot sender about 98 devices a week see the signpost (N-57) [MODEL]. Sender terms forbid joining redemptions to identity to build movement profiles (COUNSEL).
- Redeem desk, `POST /v1/conversions`, QR, per-version codes: Appendix A.

### 7.7 Billing versus display reconciliation (decision)
**Bill on released weekly cells, pooled per campaign-week, with a minimum charge and no waiver. T is for display only.**
- **Rule (D1).** For campaign c and UTC week w in which c was live on at least one day: S(c,w) = sum of the signed `noisy_value` of the C1 view cells of c's signposts live in w. `billed_views(c,w) = max(F, S(c,w))`, F = `billing_min_views` = 20 (N-1; separate key from T; not pro-rated). `charge(c,w) = min(billed_views x rate / 1000, remaining campaign budget)`, in micro-dollars, whole cents on the statement. Billing counts `markShown`-viewable events only. Minimum campaign budget $10 (N-13). No waiver, discount or per-signpost clamp.
- **Why the old waiver went.** Under the first draft's waiver (billed 0 below T) a sender is billed 0.13 of true at 5 views, 0.33 at 15, 0.67 at 20, 0.90 at 25, 0.96 at 29 (N-6); 40 signposts x 15 views are billed 198 of 600 and the best split (about 121 signposts of 5 views) is billed 0.12, 88 percent free (N-7) [MODEL]. A waiver is non-convex and rewards fragmentation.
- **Why splitting cannot help.** max(F, S) is convex and non-decreasing and the noise is zero-mean, so by Jensen E[billed] >= true for every pooling and split. E[billed]/true at V=600 (N-8): 1.000 for m = 1..100 signposts in ONE campaign; 1.011 (20), 1.394 (40), 3.359 (100) for m separate campaigns. Rejected per-signpost clamp: 1.008 (40), 1.125 (100); chain 200x5 views: clamp 1.183, per-line floor 4.025. Many tiny campaigns pay more, not less.
- **Residual error.** Under-billing: none in expectation (minimum E[billed] minus true over v=0..400 is 0.0000). Over-billing at zero deliveries is an expectation, not a maximum: for one signpost 20.045 views ($0.10 at $5 CPM, N-5); for a pooled campaign of m signposts E[max(20,S)] is 20.05 (m=1), 20.81 (5), 26.24 (25), 29.53 (40), 39.25 (100), 50.60 (200) views, i.e. $0.10 / $0.10 / $0.13 / $0.15 / $0.20 / $0.25 at $5 CPM and $0.16 to $0.40 at the $8 CPM used in section 10 [MODEL, verifier script overbill_m.py]; a single statement line can be larger (sd 7.06 x sqrt(m) views). Single-signpost excess at other true counts: +2.483 at 20, +0.914 at 25, +0.411 at 29, +0.045 at 40 (N-9). Realised weekly noise: zero-mean, sd 7.06 x sqrt(m) views for m live signposts: $0.035 (m=1), $0.50 (m=200, about 10 percent of a 1,000-view bill) (N-10). Pilot population (lognormal median 115, sigma 1.1): billed/true 1.0021, +$0.0023 per sender-week, maximum $0.095 (N-11) [MODEL; inputs U]. COUNSEL looks at charging a minimum for views that did not occur. **Declared residual, budget cap:** convexity (and so 'cannot under-bill') holds for billed_views only. charge = min(billed_views x rate / 1000, remaining budget) is not convex at the cap, so when the budget binds, E[charge] is below true: -2.48 views for one signpost, -17.8 (m=40), -28.1 (m=100), -39.8 views = -3.98 percent (m=200) at a budget of 1,000 views-equivalent [MODEL, verifier script budgetcap.py; not re-run here]. Fragmenting a budget-bound campaign across many signposts therefore earns up to about 4 percent free delivery; the alternative (cap billed_views before noise, or cap by exact count) is not adopted.
- **What the sender sees is what the sender pays.** Statement line per (campaign, week): billed_views, rate, charge, flag "minimum applied" (S < F; one bit, what the old waiver already revealed). Every line is a pure function of stored release rows (`billing_statement_line_inputs`), the public rate card and the budget; a CI test recomputes each line. Per-signpost figures appear only for a campaign-week in which every view cell is at least T (7.4 display rule); they then sum exactly to billed_views. Otherwise only the total and the minimum-applied bit appear, so the total never reveals a hidden cell. The console says "The statement total is what is charged; per-signpost figures are estimates."
- **Settlement and partner payout.** Weekly, idempotent per (campaign, week) on the sender side and per (week, partner_app) on the partner side. Partners are paid on EXACT internal counts after IVT cleaning: `payout_p = exact_p x rate x (1 - margin) / 1000`, after the payout hold. The difference to sender charges sits in a `release_float` ledger account; expected value >= 0, weekly sd about $0.35 at 100 campaigns (N-10) [MODEL arithmetic]; Proxim carries it. Pilot discounts, if any, are flat per-campaign credit lines independent of counts.
- **Pacing, budget stop and spend display (D3, I-7).** Pacing and the budget stop use exact internal counts, are never displayed, and the stop applies at the next UTC day boundary (Proxim absorbs overshoot, since charge = min(billed, remaining)). There is no spend meter, no live or in-progress number and no status from exact counts (a 10 percent meter step is 1 / 20 / 200 / 2,000 views at budgets of $0.05 / $1 / $10 / $100, an exact-count oracle, N-14; a visible pause day detects a lone commuter with accuracy 1.000 at budgets of 3 or 20 views, N-15 [MODEL]). "Spent" = settled charges only; unsettled weeks show "settling" plus "up to $X more may be charged, capped by your remaining budget" computed from the sender's own inputs. "Budget reached" is written only by the settlement job from settled charges. `delivery_budgets` and `ivt_flags` have zero sender grants. Declared residual: the internal stop's effect appears in later released cells and in the sender's own landing logs.
- **Late invalid traffic.** IVT found before release is removed from the exact aggregates that feed it. IVT found after release becomes an "invalid traffic credit" statement line = the exact IVT share rounded down to a multiple of 5 percent (zero below 5 percent, N-16) x the released charge of that campaign-week, whole cents, funded from the partner payout hold; the cube is not re-released. Disputes can only be about invalid traffic or partner fraud, not arithmetic.
- **Declines.** Raw counts shown exactly (presence oracle at 150 m rings and via decoys); the privacy report's block rounding up (systematic overcharge); "exact above k = 20" (kept: invoice equals display; dropped: exactness).
- **Independent verification, weekly (D15, N-27).** Replaces the M13 "within 1 percent from the same SDK report" and the first draft's daily 2 percent / 5 percent gate (which fails an honest partner at 0.3 opens a day: median weekly loss 6.6-6.7 percent, p95 8.0-8.1 percent, N-26 [MODEL]).
  1. **IC-A** is a weekly per-app total from the partner's own server-side pipeline, with no value passing through any Proxim SDK callback, API or TestKit, stored before and apart from Proxim ingest and labelled with its definition (`viewable_1s_50px` or `rendered`). v1 transport: a weekly CSV in the partner console (CSV due week end + 12 d). SDK-derived counts are IC-B: diagnostic only and never satisfy the gate. The attestation API and audit fleet are G-1.1S.
  2. **Reporting loss** L = 1 - E/P (E = exact deduplicated IVT-cleaned viewable events that arrived, P = IC-A) is reported per partner per week with a model band, never gated and never grossed up into billing.
  3. **Stability gate.** Four calibration weeks (c-hat = median E/P; must not be below the homogeneous-cadence model value minus 5 points), then four test weeks each within +/-10 percent of c-hat (+/-5 percent for partner-weeks of at least 1,000 events); partner-weeks under 1,000 events are pooled over 4 weeks. E/P above 1.02 is always a finding. Findings above 10 percent are investigated; above 20 percent the payout is held. Canary drives on one iPhone and one Android per partner build must deliver 0.95 to 1.00 of scripted exposures.
  4. **Performance (N-28) [MODEL]:** false-fail 0.0 percent at 150 to 2,000 devices a week; a 10 percent silent loss or duplicate is detected in 89.5 to 92.5 percent of runs, a 20 percent one in 100 percent. Inflation made before the SDK call is not detected; the controls for that are canary signposts in empty areas, the 30-day payout hold with clawback and cohort statistics.
  5. **NOT EVALUABLE.** A partner without IC-A, or with fewer than 8 weeks of data, runs in credit mode (no live money) and the gate is recorded NOT EVALUABLE, not passed. First verdict at least 70 days from first live traffic (8 weeks plus the 14-day evaluation lag); recommended pilot window 12 weeks (N-29).
  6. **Rehearsals.** A 10 percent drop, a 10 percent duplicate and a viewability regression in staging must each turn the gate script red.
  7. Other checks: staff-only cross-checks, never shown to the sender (landing sessions not above taps; redemptions on days with no deliveries); tile-fetch counts only as a coarse per-r5 anomaly check (per-partner attribution is impossible, I8). Labelled LOOSENED in 9.2 (M13-RECON).

## 9. Privacy analysis and invariant changes

### 9.1 What Proxim learns and what it never learns
Learns: IP, time and res-5 cell on tile fetches (no key, I8); IP at ingest (pre-OHTTP, not logged together with the event body; declared residual); events, from the closed I24 allowlist only: signpost, rev, type (view, tap, save, dismiss, hide), slot, UTC day, sdk/os buckets, attested flag, simulated flag. There is no wording index, no `var` and no `cta` in v1, so the server never learns which wording was shown. Never: coordinates, a persistent or cross-app identifier, hour, cell, device or session id on events, the order of one phone's events, who the user is, whether someone has two apps, or anything about customer lists (not accepted at all).

Event linkability (declared residual, PLAN 6.3 Phase 1 unchanged). The ingest token is a stateless HMAC issued after an attestation check, multi-use within one UTC day, capped at C_tok = 20 events (N-19); issuance is capped at I_tok = 1 per attestation key per UTC day, counters keyed by attestation-key hash and purged at 72 h (N-20), with no event reference. Within a day, events sharing a token or an IP are linkable by anyone who sees request logs, and the issuer can link a token to an attestation: this is policy, not cryptography, until Phase 2 (OHTTP plus blind tokens; RFC 9578 and RFC 9458 [V-sec], N-69). In v1 the linkable content is only (signpost, type, day) tuples. Phase 2 stays the hard gate for health traffic and GA, and a precondition for any history-derived field. The draft's single-use-nonce text and its "closes B2" claim are withdrawn.

Risks closed by the cut: a per-wording index would have been a persistent pseudonym (a fixed per-install rotation order is log2(6!) = 9.49 bits per family, 18.98 bits for two families [MODEL arithmetic], N-24), so `var` is not in v1. Per-recipient or per-tap values in codes, QR or URLs are barred by I21. Customer lists or personal data into Proxim or the AI vendor are not accepted (no list, CRM, custom audience, lookalike, hashed-email or lead-form feature; CSV, XLSX and VCF uploads other than the redemption CSV rejected; I25).

Other risks, stated plainly:
- **Release protection is modest.** The weekly cell is noise plus a display threshold, not differential privacy, and no privacy budget is claimed (BUDGET-LEDGER). Observer accuracy for "does a persistent +5 views a week commuter pass?" is 0.698 / 0.858 / 0.987 after 1 / 6 / 26 weeks at baselines of 20 or more; with the 20-view floor at baseline 0 it is 0.507 / 0.540 / 0.627 (N-17) [MODEL]. Strong for one-off events and lone commuters in near-empty cells, weak for regular commuters in busy cells; the lone-commuter claim holds only with the 7.4 campaign-week display rule and for observers without outside knowledge of the campaign's other signposts. Device-level sensitivity is 6 per cube-week per app, 36 over a 6-week flight, 72 with two apps (N-18). Collusion between senders and overlapping signposts are not budgeted. These numbers stay in team documents, never sender copy.
- **Budget stop and pack flags.** Pack-published stricter-only flags (pace overlay, code-hide) are one-bit functions of exact counts, public, with hysteresis (declared residual in I22). With small budgets a budget-pause day is an exact-count oracle (accuracy 1.000 at budgets of 3 or 20 views on a zero baseline, N-15), hence the $10 minimum budget (N-13) and no sender-visible meter (N-14).
- **Redemptions.** A named loyalty-card holder who redeems the family code tells the sender the person probably saw the signpost or got the code from someone who did; the code is readable from the public pack. At the median pilot sender about 98 devices a week are exposed (N-57); the code chip is hidden below 100 viewable per 14 days, which removes it from 22.5% of pilot senders and about 3.2% of expected redemptions (N-64) [MODEL]. Sender terms forbid joining redemptions to identity to build movement profiles (COUNSEL).
- The sender's landing site sees IP, time, user agent and any cookie and may infer the visitor was near the geometry (state-law "precise geolocation" radii of about 1,750 to 2,000 ft [V-sec] versus Proxim's 150 m floor; COUNSEL). The host app can read the SDK ledger (same process; disclosed). Store labels must match the real event allowlist.

## 11. AI-content policy position and COUNSEL list

**Position.** AI is assistive and untrusted. ONE server-side call drafts up to 5 wordings (plus the sender's base wording, 6 creatives in all) from the typed fact sheet, at most one repair call. The sender reviews, edits, accepts and attests each draft; the output passes the same lint, sender acceptance, attestation, human review and signature as typed text; the signature covers frozen text. Proxim does not "write your ads" and runs no AI at serve time. Text plus icon only in v1. No testimonials, reviews, ratings, endorsements, "before and after", scarcity, comparative or superlative claims; no second-person statements about a person's habits, location history, identity, health, finances, age, religion, politics or relationships; no AI images, voices or synthetic people (which keeps NY GBL 396-b, in force since 2026-06-09 [V-sec: law-firm commentary, statute text not fetched], out of scope). "Upload your campaign" in v1 means paste or type existing copy and duplicate a past campaign.
- **No on-card AI label for text.** Provenance (`source`, `ai_run_id`, model id, template hash, fact-sheet hash) is stored so a later label needs no format change. EU AI Act Art. 50 is deferred with the EU. Google Play's AI-Generated Content policy [V-sec: press coverage, page not fetchable] concerns apps that generate content; the SDK generates nothing on the device and has a Report control, but a partner may ask, so the integration guide says so.
- **What the model sees (I25).** Proxim instruction text; closed-list category, offer type, tone, goal, angle, locale; placeholder names and types; and sender-authored base wording and up to 3 short highlights after placeholder substitution, passed through a reject-not-strip validator (digit, number-word, currency, date, URL, email, @, brace, control/format/tag characters, non-allowlisted script, phone and address patterns, street-suffix words). It does not see the brand name, any fact value, targeting, pages, documents or customer data. The model writes {brand}, {price}, {offer}, {hours}, {address}, {phone}, {road_ref} placeholders (the model-writable subset of the single closed enum PlaceholderV1); the server substitutes attested values in ONE pass (values cannot contain braces); lengths are enforced after the call. The structured-output JSON schema holds only fixed enums, because the API caches the schema for up to 24 hours [V].
- **Vendor handling.** Server-side, inline text only (no Files API, no Batch API); planning model Sonnet 5.5 at $2 / $10 per MTok (N-42, [V] https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/overview, read 2026-10-10, not re-fetched by the integrator). `policy_rules.ai_models` excludes Covered Models, read from the Covered Models page at ADR time, and CI refuses any pinned model on that list. A call costs $0.0074 lean / $0.0098 planning / $0.0196 pessimistic; 100 orgs x 20 families x 2 calls is $39 a month (N-43) [MODEL arithmetic]. The vendor is a sub-processor of SENDER content (sender terms and the public sub-processor page); it receives no end-user or partner data, so the partner DPA annex is unaffected (COUNSEL). Retention: not retained by default; flagged content may be kept up to 2 years even under ZDR (N-68, [V] as tagged by the privacy specialist; the URL was not in the digest and must be re-verified before this appears in a sender document). The earlier proposed sentence ('at most 30 days in ordinary operation') is withdrawn: the vendor's primary page says conversation content is not retained by default except for Covered Models (30-day retention) and that Sonnet 5.5 is not a Covered Model [V per the scope-privacy verifier; the commercial-retention article was unreachable [U]], so '30 days' mixes the Covered Model rule into a non-covered model. Every sender disclosure must instead be copied from the vendor's current page and the signed DPA, with a retrieval date, and re-checked at each G-AI review. ZDR is a contract arrangement, not a setting, with an unknown lead time. G-AI fallback: either the owner accepts 'not retained by default; up to 2 years if flagged' without ZDR, or G-AI stays off. A switch to a Covered Model changes retention to a mandatory 30 days and re-triggers counsel; the CI denylist stays. The ZDR/DPA request needs a named owner and a date (owner decision 3). Prompt and response bodies are excluded from logs, OpenTelemetry and Sentry; `ai_runs` keeps template version, model id, input hash and token counts for 12 months.
- **Not in v1:** URL import, pasted-text or flyer/PDF extraction, the free "describe it" box, free-text targeting parse, the advisory review LLM, image generation (Appendix A; extraction needs an I25 amendment, counsel, ZDR and an ADR).

**COUNSEL list (no conclusions drawn):**
1. Whether any state law or store policy requires disclosure to end users that ad text was AI-assisted; New York GBL 396-b and EU AI Act Art. 50 (EU deferred).
2. FTC deceptive-claim, substantiation and endorsement exposure when Proxim's tool drafts the claims; the FTC consumer-reviews rule (effective about 2024-10-21 [V-sec]); creator versus publisher liability; whether the sender attestation and fact-lock suffice.

Sub-processor and DPA terms with the LLM vendor, including flagged-content retention of up to 2 years and legal holds; whether a sole proprietor's business name is personal data when sent to the vendor; and names, nicknames and place names a sender types into its own wording, which reach the vendor despite the filters.

## 14. Risks
1. "No fatigue" or "delivered every day" heard as a guarantee. Only eligibility and "never one of the last three wordings in the same app" are promised (N-38); model numbers never reach sender copy.
2. Pilot volume leaves most CTRs unreadable: CTR displayed for 63.5% of unselected senders, within +/-50% for 11.3% (N-58). The console always shows an interval; per-wording results do not exist in v1.
3. Release protection is real for one-off events and lone commuters in near-empty cells, weak for regular commuters in busy cells (N-17), and only with the 7.4 campaign-week display rule; an observer who knows the campaign's other signposts is not yet re-run; no epsilon is claimed. Mitigation: no privacy wording, G-V simulator, static allowlist.
4. Reviewers: 38 h/month at 15 senders, 188 at 100 (N-48); coverage, not hours, binds first (N-50). Mitigation: >= 2 trained reviewers, invite-only intake, tier-0 lane budget, flood control.
5. AI drift, injection, cost abuse (N-44). Mitigation: placeholders only, lint, typed serializer, quotas; AI is an untrusted author.
6. Partner inflation and unverifiable presence. Mitigation: IC-A gate, canaries, payout hold; fabrication on real users stays a residual until G-1.1S.
7. Event linkability: Phase 1 tokens are linkable within a UTC day in request logs (policy only). No history-derived field in v1; Phase 2 stays the hard gate for health and GA.
8. Cross-app repeats, reinstall and low-cadence under-reporting: best effort or disclosed.
9. Landing site as tracking surface; crawler-aware cloaking and JS-conditional redirects are declared residuals.
10. LLM vendor dependency and retention (flagged content up to 2 years, N-68). Mitigation: manual editor, pinned models, denylist CI.
11. Loyalty-card linkage of a redemption to probable exposure cannot be removed without an identifier; sender terms, COUNSEL.
12. Scope: the cut is not small (N-65 to N-67); only about 2.5 to 4 pw of levers remain.
